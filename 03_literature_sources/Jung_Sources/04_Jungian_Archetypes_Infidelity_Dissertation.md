## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This doctoral dissertation provides **crucial empirical validation** for Jungian archetypal theory in the context of committed relationships and infidelity. Using validated questionnaires with 122 adults, the research demonstrates that specific archetypal profiles (Caregiver, Innocent, Jester) significantly predict infidelity attitudes and behaviors, while meaning-in-life status mediates these relationships. The study bridges Jungian depth psychology with evolutionary psychology, proposing that archetypes represent both psychological and genetic inheritances that shape mating strategies.

**Core Relevance to Thesis:** This is the **only empirical, quantitative study** linking Jungian archetypes to mate selection and fidelity. It validates the theoretical claims of Jung's CW 9 and CW 16 with statistical evidence, making it essential for defending the integration of Jungian theory with evolutionary psychology.

## KEY FINDINGS

### 1. Caregiver and Innocent Archetypes → Lower Infidelity

**Primary Findings:**
- Individuals with strong Caregiver archetype tendencies:
  - Exhibit **lower cheating rates**
  - Express **stronger disapproval of infidelity**
  - Report higher relationship satisfaction
  
- Individuals with strong Innocent archetype tendencies:
  - Show similar patterns to Caregiver
  - Emphasize trust and loyalty
  - Less likely to be victims of infidelity

**Theoretical Explanation:** These archetypes emphasize:
- Relational investment over self-interest
- Long-term commitment over short-term gratification
- Empathy and responsibility

**Thesis Application:** Validates evolutionary psychology's concept of "slow life history strategy"—individuals with these archetypes prioritize long-term pair bonding over short-term mating. Demonstrates that psychological archetypes align with evolutionary mating strategies.

### 2. Jester Archetype → Higher Infidelity

**Primary Findings:**
- Individuals with strong Jester archetype tendencies:
  - **Higher likelihood of cheating**
  - **Higher likelihood of being cheated on**
  - More permissive attitudes toward infidelity
  - Focus on novelty and excitement

**Theoretical Explanation:** Jester archetype emphasizes:
- Novelty-seeking and stimulation
- Present-moment gratification
- Playfulness over commitment
- Emotional non-attachment

**Thesis Application:** Aligns with evolutionary psychology's "fast life history strategy"—emphasis on short-term mating, sexual variety, and reduced parental investment. Demonstrates archetypal patterns may encode evolutionary mating strategies.

### 3. Life Meaning as Mediator

**Primary Findings:**
- **High life meaning** → disapproval of infidelity, lower cheating
- **Low life meaning + high searching** → higher cheating AND being cheated on
- Meaning-in-life status interacts with archetypal patterns

**Theoretical Explanation:**
- Individuals with clear life purpose invest in long-term commitments
- Those searching for meaning may seek it through relationship novelty
- Lack of meaning creates vulnerability to impulsive mating decisions

**Thesis Application:** Links archetypal theory to existential psychology and meaning-making. Suggests contemporary mating crisis may involve both archetypal confusion AND meaning crisis—particularly relevant post-religious traditional culture.

### 4. Five+ Committed Relationships = Higher Infidelity Risk

**Primary Finding:**
- Individuals with 5+ previous committed relationships show:
  - **Increased likelihood of cheating**
  - **Increased likelihood of being cheated on**
  - Pattern suggests "serial monogamy syndrome"

**Theoretical Interpretation:**
- Serial relationship pattern may indicate:
  - Inability to integrate projections (Jung)
  - Fast life history strategy (evolutionary psychology)
  - Avoidant attachment (attachment theory)
  - Hedonic adaptation to relationships

**Thesis Application:** **CRITICAL FINDING** for Sacred Bonds thesis—serial monogamy may represent a maladaptive mating strategy that prevents archetypal integration and individuation. Modern dating culture's enabling of serial monogamy may inadvertently promote infidelity.

### 5. Archetypes More Predictive Than Demographics

**Primary Finding:**
- **Gender, race, and age were NOT significant predictors** when archetypal and meaning variables were included
- Archetypal profile was **more predictive** of infidelity than demographic factors

**Theoretical Significance:**
- Suggests universal psychological patterns (archetypes) transcend surface demographics
- Supports Jung's claim of collective unconscious as "identical in all men"
- Challenges purely social constructionist explanations of mating behavior

**Thesis Application:** Strengthens argument that mating crisis is **psychological/archetypal** rather than demographic. Solutions must address psychological integration, not merely social engineering.

## ANIMA/ANIMUS AND RELATIONSHIP DYNAMICS

### Integration vs. Non-Integration

**Key Theoretical Contribution:**

> "Failure to integrate these archetypes leads to unconscious drives and projections, potentially resulting in maladaptive relationship patterns, including infidelity. Conscious integration supports healthier mating strategies and relationship choices, promoting monogamy or conscious mate selection, while lack of integration leaves individuals vulnerable to repetitive or compulsive relationship behaviors."

**Mechanism Explained:**

**Non-Integrated Anima/Animus:**
- Individual projects anima/animus onto partners
- Partner is experienced as projection, not reality
- When projection fades, attraction vanishes
- Individual seeks new projection target (serial monogamy)
- Pattern repeats → vulnerability to infidelity

**Integrated Anima/Animus:**
- Individual recognizes anima/animus as internal
- Projection is withdrawn and integrated consciously
- Partner is experienced as real person
- Relationship based on conscious choice
- Reduced vulnerability to projection-based attraction → fidelity

**Thesis Application:** Provides psychological mechanism for why infidelity occurs even in "satisfying" relationships—the issue is not relationship quality but unconscious archetypal patterns seeking external fulfillment.

### Individuation and Relationship Success

**Key Insight:**
> "Conscious integration supports healthier mating strategies and relationship choices, promoting monogamy or conscious mate selection."

**Individuation Process in Relationships:**
1. Initial projection creates bond
2. Reality challenges projection
3. Individual must choose: flee or integrate
4. **Integration** = withdraw projection, face unconscious, relate authentically
5. Result: capacity for long-term monogamy

**Thesis Application:** Positions **individuation** as the psychological foundation for successful long-term pair bonding. Contemporary mating crisis may reflect cultural conditions that obstruct individuation (instant gratification, avoidance of psychological discomfort, dating abundance).

## EVOLUTIONARY PSYCHOLOGY + JUNGIAN THEORY INTEGRATION

### Dissertation's Theoretical Bridge

**Key Contribution:**
> "The dissertation combines evolutionary psychology (natural selection, sexual strategies) with Jungian archetypal theory, proposing that both genetic/evolutionary drives and inherited psychological archetypes contribute to behaviors like infidelity and monogamy."

**Integrated Model:**

**Evolutionary Psychology Explains:**
- Biological drives (reproduction, mate quality assessment)
- Sex differences in mating strategies
- Universal patterns across cultures
- Adaptive functions of infidelity

**Jungian Theory Explains:**
- Individual variation in mating strategies
- Psychological mechanisms of attraction
- Why some individuals are monogamous, others not
- Pathway to conscious mating choices (individuation)

**Synthesis:**
> "This integrated model frames infidelity as a co-product of evolutionary pressures and underlying archetypal influences, conceptualizing archetypes as both genetically and psychologically inherited."

**Thesis Application:** This is the **exact integration** Sacred Bonds thesis is pursuing—using Jung to explain individual/psychological variation within evolutionary patterns. Validates theoretical approach with empirical evidence.

### Archetypes as Genetic + Psychological Inheritance

**Key Theoretical Claim:**

From dissertation (p. 10):
> "Jung defined archetypes as genetically repeating characteristics pre-existing in the psyche."

From dissertation (p. 11):
> "Identifying archetypal tendencies helps connect genetic traits to choices regarding monogamy or infidelity."

From dissertation (p. 30):
> "Archetypes explain innate behaviors like infidelity."

**Implication:** Archetypes are **not merely cultural constructs** but evolutionary psychological structures—patterns that evolved because they enhanced survival/reproduction, now encoded in the collective unconscious.

**Thesis Application:** Provides textual support from Jung himself for treating archetypes as evolutionary inheritances. Counters critiques that Jungian theory is "unscientific" or "mystical."

## METHODOLOGICAL CONTRIBUTIONS

### Study Design

**Participants:** 122 adults in committed relationships  
**Instruments:**
- Validated archetypal assessment questionnaire
- Meaning in Life Questionnaire (MLQ)
- Infidelity attitudes and behaviors measures

**Analysis:** Multiple regression, correlation, factor analysis

**Strengths:**
- Quantitative validation of Jungian theory (rare)
- Uses validated instruments
- Controls for demographics
- Examines both cheating and being cheated on

**Limitations:**
- Moderate sample size (n=122)
- Self-report measures (social desirability bias)
- Cross-sectional design (cannot establish causation)
- WEIRD population (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic)

### Thesis Application

**For Sacred Bonds Methodology:**
- Model for quantitative archetypal assessment
- Demonstrates feasibility of empirical Jung research
- Provides validated instruments
- Shows archetypal theory can be tested scientifically

**Recommendations:**
