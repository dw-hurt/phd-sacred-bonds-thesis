# Larsen (2023) Comparative Analysis: Demographic Consequences of Sexual Selection

## Executive Summary

Douglas Larsen's *Biohistory: Decline and Fall of the West* (2023) provides unprecedented demographic validation of evolutionary psychology's predictions about human mating patterns. This comparative analysis examines how Larsen's 5,000-year historical dataset on polygyny and reproductive stratification validates and extends findings from Buss (2023), Firman & Simmons (2011), Gangestad & Simpson (2000), Bertrand et al., and Limar (2011). The analysis reveals that Larsen operates at the **population-demographic level**, showing the aggregate outcomes when individual-level mechanisms (documented by other sources) scale across millions of people over millennia.

**Key Finding**: Larsen's 10:1 reproductive ratio (top-decile vs. bottom-decile males) represents the *culmination* of multi-level selection processes: precopulatory filtering (Buss, Bertrand, G&S) × postcopulatory selection (Firman) × temporal aggregation (5,000 years). Individual mate preferences (psychology) → economic sorting (hypergamy) → genetic filtering (sperm competition) → demographic collapse (Larsen's endpoint).

## 3. Integration with Firman & Simmons (2011): Multiplicative Reproductive Stratification

### 3.1 Larsen's Social Selection × Firman's Genetic Selection

Larsen documents **precopulatory selection** (social mate choice). Firman documents **postcopulatory selection** (sperm competition). These operate *sequentially*, not independently.

|---------------------|-----------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Stage 1: Precopulatory** | Larsen (2023) | Female mate choice, economic hypergamy | 10:1 |

| **Combined Effect** | Integrated Model | Sequential filtering | **~30:1** |

**Calculation**:
- Top-decile male (Larsen): 10× copulation opportunities vs. bottom-decile male
- *Within* those opportunities (Firman): Genetically superior male achieves 3.3× paternity vs. control male
- **Total**: 10 × 3.3 = **33:1 reproductive advantage** for genetically superior, high-status males

### 3.2 Empirical Validation: Multiple Paternity in Humans

**Firman's Mouse Data**:
- 66% multiple paternity rate in litters
- 77% paternity monopolization by selected males
- 27-generation sustained selection pressure

**Human Analogues (Supporting Larsen × Firman Model)**:
- **Extrapair paternity (EPP)**: 2-10% of children (meta-analysis, Anderson 2006)
- **EPP partner characteristics**: Higher status, more attractive than social partner (Baker & Bellis, 1995)
- **Ovulatory cycle effects**: Women pursue higher-quality males during fertile window (Gangestad et al., 2005)

**Integration**:
1. **Larsen's mechanism**: Woman marries mid-status male (precopulatory constraint)
2. **Firman's mechanism**: Woman pursues extrapair copulation with high-status male during ovulation (postcopulatory optimization)
3. **Outcome**: Social monogamy + genetic polygyny = Larsen's 10:1 ratio sustained even in nominally "monogamous" societies

### 3.3 Temporal Dynamics: Larsen's 5,000 Years × Firman's 27 Generations

**Firman's Experimental Evolution**:
- 27 generations of selection
- Sustained 3.3:1 reproductive advantage
- No diminishing returns (selection pressure maintained)

**Larsen's Historical Timeline**:
- 5,000 years ≈ **200-250 human generations**
- Consistent 10:1 ratio across eras
- No evidence of adaptation reducing stratification

**Implication**: If Firman's 3.3:1 advantage sustains for 27 generations without equilibration, and Larsen's 10:1 ratio sustains for 250 generations, **reproductive stratification is evolutionarily stable** (not self-correcting).

**Consequence**: Modern demographic collapse (falling fertility) is *not* a temporary perturbation. It represents a stable equilibrium given current mate choice architecture + modern environmental conditions.

## 9. Theoretical Tensions and Unresolved Questions

### 9.1 Larsen's Extreme Stratification × Buss's Moderate Polygyny Assumption

**Tension**:
- **Buss**: Human evolution involved *moderate* polygyny (α ≈ 1.2-1.5)
- **Larsen**: Historical reality shows *extreme* polygyny (10:1 ratio → α ≈ 2.0-3.0)

**Question**: If Larsen's 10:1 ratio operated throughout human evolution, why does Buss assume moderate polygyny?

**Possible Resolutions**:

1. **Temporal distinction**:
   - **Buss**: Hunter-gatherer baseline (last 2 million years) = moderate polygyny
   - **Larsen**: Agricultural societies (last 10,000 years) = extreme polygyny
   - Modern psychology reflects hunter-gatherer baseline, not agricultural history

2. **Larsen's data = apex polygyny**:
   - Larsen documents *elites* (whose records survive)
   - Most males (peasants, laborers) had near-equal reproductive success
   - 10:1 ratio = top 10% vs. bottom 10%, not median vs. bottom

3. **Recent intensification**:
   - Stratification increased over time (Industrial Revolution, globalization)
   - Modern 10:1 ratio more extreme than ancestral baseline
   - Buss's moderate assumption correct for most of human evolution

**Empirical Test**: Compare reproductive stratification in:
- Hunter-gatherer societies (contemporary data)
- Early agricultural societies (archaeological genetics)
- Modern populations (Larsen's data)
- **Prediction**: Stratification should increase with societal complexity

### 9.2 Larsen's Demographic Collapse × Evolutionary Equilibrium

**Tension**:
- **Evolutionary Theory**: Natural selection should optimize for reproductive success
- **Larsen's Observation**: Modern populations exhibit *falling fertility* (TFR < 2.1) despite abundant resources

**Question**: Why doesn't evolution prevent demographic collapse?

**Possible Explanations**:

1. **Evolutionary mismatch** (Buss + Larsen):
   - Mate preferences adaptive in ancestral environment
   - Same preferences maladaptive in modern high-inequality environment
   - Insufficient time for evolutionary adaptation (<200 years)

2. **Individual optimization ≠ population optimization**:
   - Each woman optimally pursues top-decile male (individual rationality)
   - Aggregate outcome = demographic collapse (collective irrationality)
   - Classic tragedy of the commons

3. **Cultural evolution overrides biological evolution**:
   - Modern contraception, economic opportunities for women
   - Cultural values (careerism, individualism) suppress biological drives
   - Cultural change faster than biological adaptation

**Implication**: Larsen's demographic collapse may be *inevitable* outcome of evolved mate preferences + modern environmental conditions, not solvable by individual-level interventions.
