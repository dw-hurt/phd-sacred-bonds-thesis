## CITATION STYLE NOTES

### APA 7th Edition Rules for Translated Works

When citing translated Jung works:
- Include both original publication date and translation date: (Jung, 1954/1969)
- For in-text citation of only translated version: (Jung, 1969)
- In reference list, use: (Original work published 1954) after Princeton UP entry

**Example:**
Jung, C. G. (1969). *The archetypes and the collective unconscious* (R. F. C. Hull, Trans.). Princeton University Press. (Original work published 1954)

### Secondary Citations (Use Sparingly)

If citing Jung via another author:
- In-text: (Jung, 1969, as cited in Buss, 2003, p. 45)
- Reference list: Include only Buss (2003), not Jung

**Note:** Always obtain and cite primary Jung sources directly when possible.

### Multiple Works in Same Parenthesis

Alphabetical by first author's surname:
(Bowlby, 1969; Buss, 2003; Jung, 1969; Trivers, 1972)

## NOTES ON JUNG'S GENDER THEORY

### Addressing Potential Critiques

**Issue:** Jung's anima/animus theory may appear essentialist or heteronormative to contemporary readers.

**Dissertation Strategy:**
1. **Acknowledge:** Jung wrote in early-to-mid 20th century; cultural context matters
2. **Clarify:** Jung explicitly stated these are **symbolic, not biological**—"active" and "reflective" principles
3. **Modernize:** Reframe as psychological polarities (yin/yang, receptive/assertive) rather than gendered
4. **Integrate:** Show how archetypal theory complements (not contradicts) contemporary gender theory
5. **Focus on mechanism:** Emphasize projection and individuation processes, which apply regardless of gender identity

**Key Quote to Emphasize:**
> "These definitions should by no means be perceived as literally describing certain characteristics, but that these images serve as symbols and are the abstractions of active (Animus) and reflective (Anima) principles correspondingly."
