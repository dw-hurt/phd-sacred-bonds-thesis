### 2. Schmitt et al. (2004): Mate Poaching Across 53 Nations
**Category:** Evolutionary Psychology (Cross-Cultural)  
**Sample:** N=16,954 participants, 53 nations, 10 world regions  

**Key Findings:**
- 60% of men, 40% of women have attempted mate poaching
- 10% of relationships form via poaching
- Personality predictors: extraversion, disagreeableness, unconscientiousness, erotophilia
- Cultural variation: Highest in Southern Europe/South America, lowest in Africa/Asia

**Dissertation Integration:**
- Chapter 2: Evolutionary universals and cultural variation
- Chapter 6: Unconscious personality drives override conscious commitment
- Chapter 7: Mate poaching as shadow behavior (socially condemned but psychologically compelling)

**Impact:** Demonstrates unconscious factors (personality, impulsivity) override conscious rational decision-making in mate selection.

### 3. Miller (2000): Geographical Variability, Pheromones
**Category:** Evolutionary Psychology (Environmental Adaptation)  

**Key Theory:** Differential Paternal Investment Theory
- Cold climates: Male provisioning essential → monogamy, long-term bonding
- Tropical climates: Women self-sufficient → polygyny, short-term mating
- Polygyny rates correlate with latitude (confirmed)

**Pheromone Research:**
- Androstenone odor varies with women's menstrual cycle
- Symmetrical men smell better to fertile women
- Testosterone-linked pheromones signal genetic quality

**Dissertation Integration:**
- Chapter 2: Environmental evolutionary adaptation in mating strategies
- Chapter 6: Unconscious biological signals (pheromones) in mate assessment

**Impact:** Shows mating strategies shaped by ecological factors; pheromones operate completely outside conscious awareness.

### 4. Wlodarski & Dunbar (2015): Within-Sex Mating Strategy Phenotypes
**Category:** Evolutionary Psychology (Individual Differences)  

**Key Findings:**
- Two mating phenotypes within each sex (restricted/unrestricted sociosexuality)
- Bimodal distribution in behavior (SOI scores) AND biology (2D:4D digit ratios)
- Evolutionary stable strategy via frequency-dependent selection
- Biological predisposition modulated by cultural/environmental factors

**Phenotype Frequencies:**
- British Males: 43% unrestricted : 57% restricted
- US Males: 47% unrestricted : 53% restricted
- Females show similar bimodal patterns

**Dissertation Integration:**
- Chapter 2: Evolutionary basis for individual differences in pair bonding
- Chapter 3: Two phenotypes as archetypal polarities (commitment vs. freedom)
- Chapter 6: Biological unconscious (prenatal testosterone/2D:4D)
- Chapter 8: Clinical phenotype assessment in couples counseling

**Impact:** Explains why "one-size-fits-all" models fail; provides biological basis for individual variation in bonding propensity.
