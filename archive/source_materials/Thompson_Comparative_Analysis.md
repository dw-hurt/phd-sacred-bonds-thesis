# Comparative Analysis: Thompson - Digital Platforms and Non-Monogamy

**Primary Source:** Thompson, R. (In press). More than the selfie: online dating, non-monogamy, normativity, and linked profiles on OkCupid. *Journal of Language and Sexuality*.

---

## Overview

Thompson's qualitative analysis of OkCupid's interface design provides a **critical cultural and technological perspective** on contemporary mating markets that complements the evolutionary, psychological, and demographic analyses in other dissertation sources. Where sources like Firman & Larsen focus on biological mechanisms and Bertrand et al. examine economic factors, Thompson illuminates how **digital architecture shapes which mating strategies are viable** in practice.

**Key Contribution:** Demonstrates that technology is not a neutral solution to mating market problems—platform design reflects and reinforces cultural assumptions about "normal" relationships, even while appearing inclusive.

---

## 1. COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS WITH OTHER DISSERTATION SOURCES

### vs. Firman & Larsen (2011): Evolutionary Mechanisms

#### Points of Convergence
- **Mating Strategy Diversity:** Both acknowledge humans exhibit diverse mating patterns
  - **Firman & Larsen:** Focus on sperm competition and biological adaptations for both monogamous and multi-partner mating
  - **Thompson:** Empirical evidence that 83% of human cultures practice some form of non-monogamy
  - **Integration:** Thompson shows cultural/technological manifestation of the biological flexibility Firman & Larsen describe

#### Points of Divergence
- **Level of Analysis:**
  - **Firman & Larsen:** Biological mechanisms (testis size, sperm morphology, copulatory plugs)
  - **Thompson:** Cultural/technological mechanisms (interface design, discourse, self-presentation)
- **Complementarity:** Understanding biological capacity for mating diversity doesn't explain which strategies are culturally sanctioned—Thompson fills this gap

#### Synthesis for Dissertation
Use Firman & Larsen to establish **biological capacity** for diverse mating strategies, then use Thompson to explain why **digital platforms constrain** this biological flexibility through design choices that privilege monogamy and hierarchical couple structures.

---

### vs. Gangestad & Simpson (2000): Individual Differences in Mating Strategies

#### Points of Convergence
- **Strategic Pluralism:** Both recognize humans employ diverse mating tactics
  - **Gangestad & Simpson:** Focus on individual variation in short-term vs. long-term strategies, influenced by developmental context and environmental factors
  - **Thompson:** Documents how individuals navigate digital spaces to signal diverse relationship preferences
  - **Shared Insight:** Mating strategy is flexible, not fixed

#### Points of Divergence
- **Individual vs. Structural Focus:**
  - **Gangestad & Simpson:** Emphasize individual differences (personality, life history, environmental cues)
  - **Thompson:** Emphasizes **structural constraints** imposed by platform architecture
- **Missing Link:** Gangestad & Simpson assume individuals can freely express mating strategies; Thompson shows technological gatekeeping limits this expression

#### Synthesis for Dissertation
Gangestad & Simpson explain **why individuals vary** in mating strategies; Thompson reveals **how digital platforms mediate** (and distort) the expression of these strategies. This creates a **strategy-expression gap** contributing to contemporary mating difficulties.

**Example:** An individual oriented toward polyamory (Gangestad & Simpson's framework) faces platform design that only allows linking one partner (Thompson's finding), creating frustration and limiting viable strategy expression.

---

### vs. Buss (2023): Evolutionary Psychology of Mate Selection

#### Points of Convergence
- **Mate Preferences and Signaling:** Both analyze how individuals signal desirability
  - **Buss:** Focuses on evolved mate preferences (physical attractiveness, resources, kindness)
  - **Thompson:** Analyzes how these preferences are **communicated through digital profiles** (photos, text, emojis, menus)
  - **Connection:** Digital platforms become the medium through which Buss's evolved preferences are expressed

#### Points of Divergence
- **Universal vs. Constructed:**
  - **Buss:** Emphasizes universal, evolved mate preferences
  - **Thompson:** Highlights how **platform design constructs** what can be preferred/signaled
- **Critical Insight:** Even if Buss is correct about universal preferences, Thompson shows these preferences **cannot be authentically expressed** when platforms limit options

#### Synthesis for Dissertation
Use Buss to establish **what people want** in mates (evolved preferences), then use Thompson to analyze **how digital constraints prevent** authentic preference expression. This misalignment contributes to mating market dysfunction.

**Example:** Buss identifies preference for committed partners; Thompson shows platforms reinforce this through singular "partner" language—but this marginalizes people preferring multiple committed relationships.

---

### vs. Bertrand et al. (2023): Demographic and Economic Factors

#### Points of Convergence
- **Market Imbalances:** Both identify structural mating market problems
  - **Bertrand et al.:** Focus on sex ratio imbalances, education mismatches, economic inequality
  - **Thompson:** Analyzes how technology responds to (and shapes) these market dynamics
  - **Shared Concern:** Market-level factors (not just individual choices) drive mating outcomes

#### Points of Divergence
- **Economic vs. Cultural Mechanisms:**
  - **Bertrand et al.:** Emphasize quantitative, economic variables (earnings, education, supply/demand)
  - **Thompson:** Examines **qualitative, cultural** factors (normativity, discourse, identity)
- **Complementarity:** Bertrand identifies *who* is disadvantaged in mating markets; Thompson explains *how* platform design perpetuates these disadvantages

#### Synthesis for Dissertation
Bertrand et al. document **economic/demographic crises**; Thompson shows how **digital platforms fail to solve** (and may worsen) these crises by encoding restrictive norms. Together, they reveal a **multi-level crisis** (structural + cultural + technological).

**Example:** Bertrand finds educated women face fewer available partners → they turn to dating apps → Thompson shows apps still privilege traditional gender/relationship norms → crisis persists despite technological "solution."

---

### vs. Larsen (2023): Technology and Alienation

#### Points of Convergence ⭐⭐⭐ (Strongest Alignment)
- **Technology Critique:** Both skeptical of digital platforms as solutions
  - **Larsen:** Argues technology creates alienation, commodification, and authenticity loss
  - **Thompson:** Provides **empirical evidence** for Larsen's theoretical concerns
  - **Mutual Reinforcement:** Thompson's data validates Larsen's philosophical critique

#### Key Alignments
1. **Authenticity Crisis:**
   - **Larsen:** Technology mediates and distorts authentic relating
   - **Thompson:** Platform constraints create gap between authentic desires and allowable self-presentation
   
2. **Commodification:**
   - **Larsen:** Mating becomes transactional in digital markets
   - **Thompson:** Profile curation as "technologies of the self" in competitive spaces

3. **Normative Rigidity:**
   - **Larsen:** Technology reinforces dominant cultural patterns
   - **Thompson:** Despite inclusive surface features, platforms reinscribe mononormativity

#### Points of Divergence
- **Theory vs. Empirical Evidence:**
  - **Larsen:** Philosophical/theoretical analysis
  - **Thompson:** Qualitative data (interviews, profile analysis)
- **Strength:** Together they provide both theoretical framework and empirical validation

#### Synthesis for Dissertation
Larsen provides the **theoretical lens** (technology as alienating force); Thompson supplies **concrete examples** of how this operates (linking features, menu constraints). Use Thompson's findings as **case studies** supporting Larsen's broader argument.

## 2. AREAS OF SUPPORT FOR DISSERTATION THESIS

### Strong Support Areas

#### A. Technology Shapes Mating Market Norms
**Thompson's Evidence:**
- Platforms with inclusive menus increase CNM visibility
- "Visibility creates acceptance" (Rudder, cited p.14)
- Adding labels to mainstream platforms shifts perceptions of "normal"

**How It Supports Dissertation:**
- Validates technology as **active force** shaping mating norms, not passive tool
- Shows possibility of **positive intervention** through design changes
- Provides empirical case of technology reducing stigma for alternative mating strategies

**Integration:** Use in Chapter 4 to argue digital platforms are **critical intervention points** for addressing contemporary mating crisis.

---

#### B. Incomplete Solutions Perpetuate Crisis
**Thompson's Evidence:**
- Despite inclusive features, platforms reinforce mononormativity through:
  - Limiting profile linking to one partner
  - Defaulting to singular "partner" language
  - Privileging couple-centered photos
- Creates **visibility without full legitimization**

**How It Supports Dissertation:**
- Demonstrates **superficial changes insufficient** to address deep cultural patterns
- Shows technology can simultaneously empower AND marginalize
- Validates need for **multi-dimensional framework** (not just technological fixes)

**Integration:** Use in Chapter 8 (Synthesis) to argue that addressing mating crisis requires **cultural transformation**, not just better apps.

---

#### C. Mating Strategy Diversity is Biologically Normal
**Thompson's Evidence:**
- Only 17% of human cultures practice strict monogamy (Chapais 2013)
- 21% of U.S. adults report CNM experience (Haupert et al. 2017)
- At least 1.44 million U.S. polyamorous adults (Rubel & Burleigh 2020)

**How It Supports Dissertation:**
- Counters monogamy-as-universal assumption
- Provides **cross-cultural and contemporary** evidence for mating flexibility
- Supports evolutionary psychology premise that humans have diverse mating repertoire

**Integration:** Use in Chapter 1 and Chapter 7 to establish **empirical foundation** for discussing alternative relationship structures.

---

#### D. Authenticity Crisis in Digital Age
**Thompson's Evidence:**
- Gap between authentic desires and platform-allowable expression
- Profile creation as constrained meaning-making
- Users must "negotiate affordances and constraints" to represent selves

**How It Supports Dissertation:**
- Documents **specific mechanisms** creating authenticity crisis
- Shows crisis is **structural** (platform design), not just individual failure
- Validates Jungian/transpersonal concerns about authentic Self-expression

**Integration:** Use to support archetypal analysis—platforms constrain individuation by limiting authentic self-expression.

---

#### E. Thin Markets Drive Platform Adoption
**Thompson's Evidence:**
- LGBTQ+ adults use platforms at nearly 2x rate of heterosexuals (55% vs. 28%)
- People in "thin markets" especially rely on online dating (Rosenfeld & Thomas)

**How It Supports Dissertation:**
- Explains **why certain groups** disproportionately affected by platform design
- Shows technology is **not optional** for marginalized groups—it's necessity
- Validates focus on digital platforms as critical research site

**Integration:** Use to argue that fixing digital platforms is **justice issue**, not just convenience—marginalized groups have no alternative.

## 3. AREAS OF COUNTERARGUMENT OR ALTERNATIVE PERSPECTIVE

### Potential Tensions

#### A. Sample Limitations
**Thompson's Constraint:**
- Sample primarily white, middle-class, educated, LGBTQ+/CNM-identified
- Focused on OkCupid specifically

**Implication for Dissertation:**
- Findings may not generalize to:
  - Heterosexual monogamous users
  - Working-class populations
  - Users of other platforms (Tinder, Bumble, Feeld)
- **Response:** Argue Thompson's findings are **proof of concept**—if even "inclusive" platforms have problems, mainstream platforms likely worse

---

#### B. Potential Over-Emphasis on Technology
**Thompson's Focus:**
- Primarily examines platform design and discourse
- Less attention to offline social factors, economic structures, or biological constraints

**Implication for Dissertation:**
- Could be read as **technological determinism**
- **Response:** Frame Thompson as examining **one dimension** of multi-dimensional crisis (technology + economics + biology + culture)

---

#### C. CNM-Centric vs. General Mating Crisis
**Thompson's Scope:**
- Specifically analyzes non-monogamous user experiences
- May not capture mainstream monogamous users' challenges

**Implication for Dissertation:**
- Need to carefully bridge from CNM-specific findings to general mating market dysfunction
- **Response:** Argue that CNM users are **canaries in coal mine**—their struggles reveal platform limitations affecting everyone

## 4. HOW THOMPSON EXTENDS EXISTING LITERATURE

### Methodological Contributions

#### Multimodal Critical Discourse Analysis (MCDS)
- **Innovation:** Integrates analysis of text, images, interface design, and user practices
- **Advantage:** Captures how meaning is made across multiple semiotic modes
- **Application:** Can be applied to other platforms (Tinder, Bumble) or other digital spaces (social media, forums)

#### Insider Positionality
- **Innovation:** Researcher's membership in CNM/LGBTQ+ communities enables nuanced interpretation
- **Advantage:** Access to insider knowledge, community trust, authentic data
- **Limitation:** Requires reflexive awareness of bias

---

### Theoretical Contributions

#### Expanding Queer Linguistics
- **Innovation:** Applies queer linguistics framework to multimodal digital spaces
- **Extends:** Traditional queer linguistics focused on language; Thompson adds visual/design analysis
- **Impact:** Opens new research avenue for studying sexuality/normativity in digital contexts

#### Mononormativity Concept
- **Innovation:** Operationalizes mononormativity through concrete platform features
- **Extends:** Previous work was more theoretical; Thompson shows **how mononormativity is enacted** through design
- **Impact:** Makes abstract concept measurable and actionable

---

### Empirical Contributions

#### Platform Evolution Documentation
- **Innovation:** Chronicles OkCupid's design changes over time (2014-2016+)
- **Value:** Provides historical context for understanding current digital landscape
- **Application:** Model for longitudinal studies of platform evolution

#### Linking Feature Analysis
- **Innovation:** First detailed analysis of profile linking feature
- **Finding:** Shows how features designed to include can simultaneously marginalize
- **Impact:** Reveals **unintended consequences** of design choices

## 5. GAPS AND LIMITATIONS

### What Thompson Doesn't Address

#### 1. Quantitative Outcomes
- **Gap:** No data on whether platform features affect relationship formation rates, satisfaction, or longevity
- **Implication:** Can't directly measure **impact** of design on relationship outcomes
- **Dissertation Strategy:** Pair Thompson's qualitative insights with quantitative data from other sources (Bertrand et al., Gangestad & Simpson)

#### 2. Cross-Platform Comparison
- **Gap:** Focus on OkCupid; doesn't compare to Tinder, Bumble, Feeld, etc.
- **Implication:** Unknown if findings generalize to other platforms
- **Dissertation Strategy:** Note as limitation; call for future research

#### 3. Economic Analysis
- **Gap:** Limited attention to business model, monetization, or commercial incentives shaping design
- **Implication:** May underestimate profit motive vs. social inclusion in platform decisions
- **Dissertation Strategy:** Supplement with critical analysis of platform business models (e.g., "lifelong daters" more profitable)

#### 4. User Resistance and Agency
- **Gap:** Focus on constraints; less on how users creatively resist or work around limitations
- **Implication:** May overstate deterministic effect of platform design
- **Dissertation Strategy:** Balance with examples of user agency, hacking, alternative platforms

#### 5. Evolutionary Psychology Integration
- **Gap:** Limited engagement with evolutionary explanations for why mononormativity persists
- **Implication:** Cultural analysis doesn't explain **why** pair-bonding scripts are so resilient
- **Dissertation Strategy:** Use evolutionary psychology (Gangestad & Simpson, Buss) to explain ultimate causes; Thompson explains proximate cultural mechanisms

## 6. INTEGRATION STRATEGY FOR DISSERTATION

### Multi-Level Analysis Framework

```
LEVEL 1: Biological/Evolutionary Foundations
↓ (Firman & Larsen, Gangestad & Simpson, Buss)
└─ Humans have evolved capacity for diverse mating strategies

LEVEL 2: Cultural/Normative Layer
↓ (Thompson, Larsen)
└─ Culture privileges monogamy despite biological flexibility

LEVEL 3: Economic/Structural Constraints
↓ (Bertrand et al.)
└─ Market imbalances create mate scarcity

LEVEL 4: Technological Mediation
↓ (Thompson, Larsen)
└─ Digital platforms reflect/reinforce cultural norms, creating expression gap

RESULT: Contemporary Mating Crisis
└─ Biological capacity → Cultural constraint → Economic scarcity → Technological gatekeeping
```

### Sacred Bonds Framework Mapping

#### Evolutionary Foundations Dimension
- **Thompson's Contribution:** Empirical evidence (17% monogamy cross-culturally) supports evolutionary flexibility
- **Integration:** Use to validate that Sacred Bonds framework must accommodate mating diversity

#### Archetypal Dimension
- **Thompson's Contribution:** Platform design embodies archetypal couple scripts (Anima/Animus integration through pair-bonding)
- **Integration:** Analyze mononormativity as **cultural complex** requiring conscious integration

#### Contemporary Crisis Dimension
- **Thompson's Contribution:** Authenticity gap between desires and platform-allowable expression
- **Integration:** Frame as **individuation crisis**—technology prevents Self-actualization

#### Synchronicity Dimension
- **Thompson's Contribution:** "Visibility creates acceptance"—platform menus create legitimacy fields
- **Integration:** Technological affordances as **synchronistic structures** shaping collective consciousness

## 9. CRITICAL EVALUATION

### Strengths
✅ **Rigorous qualitative methodology** (interviews + profile analysis + observation)  
✅ **Insider positionality** enables nuanced interpretation  
✅ **Multimodal analysis** captures complexity of digital self-presentation  
✅ **Theory-data integration** (MCDS + queer linguistics + mononormativity)  
✅ **Practical implications** for platform design improvements  

### Limitations
⚠️ **Sample demographics** (white, middle-class, educated) limit generalizability  
⚠️ **Single platform focus** (OkCupid) may not represent entire digital landscape  
⚠️ **No outcome data** (can't measure impact on relationship formation/satisfaction)  
⚠️ **Limited evolutionary integration** (doesn't explain why mononormativity persists)  
⚠️ **Pre-publication** status (peer review may refine arguments)  

### Overall Assessment
**Highly valuable** source that fills critical gap in dissertation's multi-level analysis. Provides concrete, empirical grounding for theoretical concerns about technology's role in contemporary mating crisis. Best used in combination with evolutionary (Firman & Larsen, Gangestad & Simpson) and economic (Bertrand et al.) perspectives to create comprehensive picture.
