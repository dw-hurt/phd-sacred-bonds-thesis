## Abstract Summary

Larsen argues that sex ratio theory explains dysfunctional mating practices in the West and predicts demographic collapse. Using Nordic countries (world's most gender-equal region) as primary case study, he documents:
- 23+ nations on course for 50% population reduction by 2100
- Scandinavia: 43-46% one-person households (world's highest)
- Norwegian fertility: 2.0 → 1.5 in one decade
- Perceived sex ratio (PSR) activates "polygynous mindset" in prosperous monogamous societies
- Promiscuity increases while pair-bonding decreases

**Thesis:** Legalizing polygyny could counter demographic collapse by aligning legal structures with evolved psychology, though with significant trade-offs including male competition and social instability.

**Methodology:** 
- Evolutionary literary criticism analyzing 1,000 years of Nordic literature
- Integration of sex ratio theory with demographic data
- Biocultural approach connecting hominin evolution to modern mating patterns

## Theoretical Framework

### Sex Ratio Theory - Core Concepts

**1. Perceived Sex Ratio (PSR) vs. Adult Sex Ratio (ASR)**
- ASR: Raw ratio of reproductive-age men to women
- PSR: Subjective perception of available *acceptable* partners
- PSR more predictive of mating behavior than ASR
- Female hypergamy narrows PSR even when ASR is balanced

**2. Alternative Sex Ratio Hypothesis**
- Rejects Classical Hypothesis (both sexes lower standards when scarce)
- **Low-ratio context (surplus women):**
  - Men lower standards, increase promiscuity
  - Women raise standards, increase selectivity (paradox)
  - "Polygynous mindset" activated in women
  - Women compete via early pregnancy, sexualized displays
  - Men prefer short-term mating over commitment

- **High-ratio context (surplus men):**
  - Men increase investment in pair-bonds
  - Men compete economically rather than violently
  - Earlier marriage, higher male wages
  - Reduced promiscuity, increased fertility
  - Greater female well-being

**3. Evoked vs. Transmitted Culture (Cosmides & Tooby, 1992)**
- **Transmitted culture:** Learned norms (monogamy laws, romantic love ideology)
- **Evoked culture:** Universal psychological mechanisms responding to environment
- Mating strategies are evoked by local conditions (prosperity, sex ratio)
- Monogamy norms = transmitted; polygynous psychology = evoked
- Mismatch drives contemporary dysfunction

## Literary-Historical Analysis

### Mating Ideology Evolution (Past 1,000 Years)

**1. Heroic Love (Viking Age: 750-1050)**
- Woman submits to superior warrior
- Polygyny, concubinage, sexual slavery widespread
- Rape justified by kinship morality (us vs. them)

**2. Courtly Love (1200s)**
- Male earns female consent through chivalry
- Introduced by King Hakon via Tristan romances (1226)
- Transition from heroic rape culture to female consent
- Replaced heroic ideology across Norse aristocracy

**3. Companionate Love (1500-1700s)**
- Pragmatic partnership for household survival
- European Marriage Pattern (EMP): Late marriage (mid-20s)
- Neolocal residence after resource accumulation
- Suppressed evolved impulses for serial pair-bonding

**4. Romantic Love (1770-1960s)**
- First Sexual Revolution (1770): Individual choice
- Sacralization of pair-bond ("merging" of two halves)
- Illegitimacy doubled (England), quadrupled (France/Germany)
- Drove 19th-century population explosion

**5. Libertine Love (Transitional, 1700s-1800s)**
- Pleasure-seeking without commitment
- Proto-romantic female sexuality acceptance
- Celebrated in Bellman's *Fredman's Epistles* (1790)

**6. Confluent Love (1960s-Present)**
- Second Sexual Revolution (1960s)
- Pair-bond lasts only while emotionally/materially beneficial
- Sacralization of self-realization and convenience
- Justifies voluntary childlessness
- Return to serial pair-bonding (evolved baseline)

**Key Literary Sources Analyzed:**
- Icelandic sagas (1200s)
- *Tristrams saga ok Ísöndar* (1226)
- *The Unfaithful Wife* (c. 1500)
- Ludvig Holberg comedies (1720s)
- Carl Michael Bellman, *Fredman's Epistles* (1790)
- Henrik Ibsen and August Strindberg (1800s)

## Hominin Mating Evolution Summary

**6+ Million Years Ago: CHLCA (Chimpanzee-Human Last Common Ancestor)**
- Multi-male/multi-female promiscuous groups
- High-status males favored for reproduction
- <10% of mammals pair-bond; chimps/bonobos do not

**4-2 Million Years Ago: Transition to Polygyny**
- Biparental care evolved with longer offspring dependence
- Males provisioned females, kept harems for paternity certainty
- Neurobiological pair-bonding capacity evolved (oxytocin, vasopressin)
- Female bias for high-status males instilled during this period

**~2 Million Years Ago: Transition to Monogamy (Genus *Homo*)**
- Ecological pressures: Larger brain = more calories = dispersed females
- Tools/weapons equalized power, limited harem defense
- Low-status males innovated: Resources for sexual exclusivity
- Canine reduction, decreased sexual dimorphism
- Female trade-off: Genetic quality (high-status genes) vs. provisioning

**~10,000 Years Ago: Neolithic Return to Polygyny**
- Agriculture enabled extreme wealth accumulation
- Elite males built harems (hundreds/thousands of women)
- 80%+ of cultures practiced polygyny historically
- Kinship societies: War motivated by female capture

**~500-1500 CE: Christian Monogamy Imposition**
- Church dissolved European tribal polygyny (4th century onward)
- Monogamy = group selection advantage (Henrich, 2020)
- Psychological-institutional coevolution → modernity
- European Marriage Pattern (EMP) emerged

**Present: De Facto Polygyny Under Legal Monogamy**
- Temporal polygyny: Serial relationships with high-value males
- Female recycling of elite males
- Low-status male marginalization
- Return to serial pair-bonding (evolutionary baseline)

## Policy Proposal and Implications

### Case for Legalizing Polygyny

**Potential Benefits:**
1. **Increased fertility** - Primary goal to counter demographic collapse
2. **Reduced singlehood** - Women can share high-value males formally
3. **Stable multi-parent households** - Prevents family breakup from serial monogamy
4. **Intact families** - Children maintain relationships with all parents
5. **High-ratio psychological effects:**
   - Men perceive scarcity, increase prosocial behavior
   - Economic competition replaces violence
   - Greater male investment in pair-bonds
   - Earlier marriage, higher male wages
   - Reduced promiscuity

**Potential Costs:**
1. **Intensified male competition** - Could increase violence, social instability
2. **Low-status male exclusion** - Already occurring, but could worsen
3. **Patriarchal structures** - Risk to women's autonomy
4. **Reduced paternal investment** - Men prioritize mating effort over parenting
5. **Economic inefficiency** - Men compete for wives rather than productive work

**Larsen's Argument:**
- Low-status men already marginalized (23% childless, 45% single)
- De facto polygyny already exists through temporal relationships
- Legal polygyny merely formalizes current reality
- High singlehood (33%) = already "polygynous" environment
- Trade-off: Accept costs to gain fertility increase

**Alternative Measures Insufficient:**
- Immigration: Norway tripled immigrants (2000-2015), fertility still fell
- Pro-natalist policies: Financial incentives, childcare subsidies failed
- Gender equality paradox: Nordic model failed to sustain fertility

## 2011 Canadian Legal Case Context

### Joseph Henrich vs. Todd Shackelford Expert Testimony

**Henrich (Against Legalization):**
- Rapid transformation risk: 70% of female undergrads prefer being second wife of billionaire over exclusive wife of average man
- Polygyny would spread via high-status role modeling
- Immigration from polygynous cultures would normalize practice
- Predicted harms: Earlier female marriage, larger age gaps, husband control, reduced paternal investment, GDP decline, increased fertility (viewed as negative)

**Shackelford (For Legalization):**
- All mating systems have negative correlates (monogamy → jealousy, violence)
- Henrich extrapolates without accounting for cultural context
- Polygyny spread "terribly, terribly unlikely" in modern Canada
- Modern polygamy can empower women

**Court Decision (BCSC 1588, 2011):**
- Upheld polygamy prohibition (up to 5 years prison)
- Adopted Rose McDermott position: Harms universal regardless of context
- Similar U.S. outcome: *Sister Wives* case rejected (2017)

**Shifting Cultural Attitudes:**
- Americans viewing polygamy as morally acceptable: 7% (2003) → 23% (2022)
- Canadians supporting decriminalization: 36% (2018)
- Several U.S. cities recognized polygamous domestic partnerships (2020s)

## Integration with Other Sources

### Connections to Gangestad & Simpson (2000)

**Strategic Pluralism Theory Validation:**
- Larsen provides empirical support for environmental condition → mating strategy shifts
- Prosperity activates specific strategies (polygynous mindset in women)
- PSR operates as environmental cue triggering strategy change
- Both short-term and long-term strategy activation documented
- Low PSR → dual strategy: Increased promiscuity + increased selectivity (paradox explained)

**Extending G&S Framework:**
- G&S focused on individual differences; Larsen adds population-level dynamics
- G&S discussed pathogen stress; Larsen adds economic prosperity/gender equality
- Both confirm: Mating strategies are facultative, not fixed

### Connections to Buss (1989, 2003, 2016)

**Universal Preferences Persistence:**
- Female preferences for status, resources, dominance remain constant
- Economic independence doesn't eliminate preferences, just changes trade-offs
- Hypergamy persists even in most gender-equal societies (Nordic data)
- Male provisioning less necessary → women more selective, not less
- Validates Buss's cross-cultural preference findings in prosperity context

**Sex Differences in Mating Psychology:**
- Male bias for polygyny/promiscuity (never selected against)
- Female preference hierarchy: Status > commitment when economically independent
- Partner variety preference asymmetry confirmed (Clark & Hatfield paradigm)

### Connections to Bertrand et al. (2021)

**Gender Gap Reversal → Mating Market Effects:**
- Bertrand: Economic empowerment → reduced marriage rates

- Both document: Prosperity paradox (equality → singlehood)

**Educational Assortative Mating:**

- Mechanism: Female economic independence → stricter mate filtering

## Methodological Approach

**Biocultural Analysis:**
- Evolutionary psychology + literary criticism hybrid
- Similar to Nordlund (2007) on Shakespeare's love forms
- Uses fiction as window into evolved psychology + cultural transmission

**Strengths:**
1. **Longitudinal cultural data** - 1,000 years of documented ideology shifts
2. **Natural experiment** - Nordic region as test case for gender equality effects
3. **Multi-level analysis** - Biological evolution + cultural evolution + individual psychology
4. **Hard demographic data** - Registry data from Norway, Denmark, Finland
5. **Cross-cultural validation** - Compares Nordic, U.S., and Asian trends

**Limitations:**
1. **Correlation vs. causation** - Prosperity + gender equality confounded
2. **Literary interpretation** - Subjective reading of ideological content
3. **Projection uncertainty** - Cannot predict actual polygyny uptake if legalized
4. **Policy extrapolation** - Limited experimental data on modern polygyny outcomes
5. **Male perspective underexplored** - Focuses more on female mate choice psychology

## Critical Evaluation

### Strengths

**1. Empirical Grounding:**
- Exceptional use of Nordic registry data (most reliable demographic sources)
- Quantifies male stratification with precision (90% vs. 40% pair-bonding)
- U.S. sexual inactivity trends provide cross-cultural validation
- Involuntary childlessness gender gap (3:1 ratio) is striking finding

**2. Theoretical Integration:**
- Successfully bridges evolutionary psychology, demography, literary criticism
- PSR concept resolves paradoxes (selectivity + promiscuity coexist)
- Evoked vs. transmitted culture framework is powerful explanatory tool
- Sex ratio theory application to prosperity contexts is novel

**3. Historical Depth:**
- Traces 6 million years of hominin mating evolution
- 1,000-year cultural analysis shows ideology-behavior coevolution
- Documents five distinct mating ideology transitions
- Shows how MFPs (marriage and family practices) shaped modernity

**4. Policy Courage:**
- Willing to propose controversial solution (polygyny legalization)
- Acknowledges trade-offs honestly (low-status male costs)
- Balances evolutionary logic with social consequences
- Engages seriously with Henrich vs. Shackelford debate

### Weaknesses

**1. Causal Ambiguity:**
- Prosperity + gender equality + welfare confounded (can't isolate drivers)
- Contraception availability not fully addressed (enables promiscuity without pregnancy)
- Cultural globalization effects underexplored (social media, dating apps)
- Testosterone decline in males mentioned but not integrated

**2. Polygyny Prediction Uncertainty:**
- Limited data on modern polygyny outcomes in wealthy societies
- Henrich's 70% female undergrad preference may not translate to behavior
- Doesn't address polyandry possibility (dismissed too quickly as ~1% of cultures)
- Assumes polygyny would primarily be formal vs. continued temporal

**3. Male Psychology Undertheorized:**
- Female mate choice psychology extensively analyzed
- Male response to marginalization less developed (Incel culture mentioned but not deeply explored)
- Male PSR effects not symmetrically analyzed
- Low-status male solutions beyond "ideological opt-out" underdeveloped

**4. Alternative Explanations:**
- Doesn't fully engage with economic anxiety explanations (housing costs, precarity)
- Social media effects on mate perception underexplored
- Porn availability as male sexual outlet not integrated
- Climate anxiety, existential dread (Bainbridge's concerns) mentioned but not analyzed

**5. Literary Method Limitations:**
- Fiction reflects and shapes ideology, but causal direction unclear
- Elite literature may not represent common mating practices
- Interpretation of mating ideology from texts requires subjective judgment
- Sampling limited to Nordic literature (though justified for case study)

## Research Notes

**Key Insights for Dissertation:**

1. **PSR as Explanatory Variable:** Most compelling contribution is perceived sex ratio (PSR) as mechanism linking prosperity to mating dysfunction. Women may face 1:1 ASR but perceive scarcity of *acceptable* mates, triggering low-ratio psychology designed for polygynous contexts.

2. **Evoked Culture Framework:** Cosmides & Tooby's distinction resolves nature/culture debate. Monogamy = transmitted (learned norms), polygynous psychology = evoked (innate responses to environment). Mismatch drives contemporary problems.

3. **Empirical Precision:** Nordic registry data provides gold-standard demographic evidence. 50-percentage-point pair-bonding gap by salary (90% vs. 40%) is most precise quantification of male stratification in corpus.

4. **Ideological Evolution:** 1,000-year literary analysis shows mating ideologies coevolve with economic systems. Each transition (heroic → courtly → companionate → romantic → confluent) adapted psychology to new ecology. Confluent love = first ideology that *doesn't* motivate reproduction.

5. **Policy Realism:** Willing to propose politically taboo solution (polygyny) while acknowledging costs. Rare example of evolutionary psychologist engaging directly with policy trade-offs rather than just describing mechanisms.

**Integration Strategy:**

- **Combine with Gangestad & Simpson:** Larsen provides population-level validation of G&S's Strategic Pluralism Theory. PSR = environmental cue activating facultative strategies.

- **Extend Bertrand:** Larsen gives evolutionary explanation for Bertrand's economic findings. Gender gap → economic independence → hypergamy → singlehood = complete causal chain.

- **Ground Buss:** Larsen shows Buss's universal preferences play out in prosperity by *increasing* female selectivity, not decreasing. Economic independence doesn't eliminate evolved preferences.

- **Operationalize Jung:** Animus projection = psychological mechanism underlying PSR perception. Archetypal ideals → narrow acceptable mate pool → low PSR experience.

**Methodological Lessons:**

- Literary analysis = underutilized data source for cultural evolution
- Registry data > survey data for demographic patterns
- Long time horizons (millions of years) help explain modern phenomena
- Interdisciplinary integration (evolution + literature + demography) yields novel insights
