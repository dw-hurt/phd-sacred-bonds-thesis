## 🎯 THEORETICAL FOUNDATION

### **QUOTE 1: Central Thesis - Strategic Pluralism**
> "This article proposes a contextual-evolutionary theory of human mating strategies. Both men and women are hypothesized to have evolved distinct psychological mechanisms that underlie short-term and long-term strategies... Different adaptive problems must be solved when pursuing a short-term as opposed to a long-term sexual strategy."

**Context:** Introduction to Sexual Strategies Theory  
**Significance:** Establishes **strategic pluralism** - both sexes have multiple context-dependent strategies  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3 Section 3.2 (SST Framework), Chapter 2 (theoretical foundation)

---

### **QUOTE 2: Desire as Causal Foundation**
> "According to this theory, desire lies at the foundation of sexuality and human mating. The focus of the theory, therefore, centers on identifying desires and all manifold consequences of desires. The desires of one sex, for example, determine which tactics of attraction are effective when used by the opposite sex. The pursuit of a desire by one sex that interferes with the desires of the opposite sex, to take another example, is the major source of conflict between the sexes."

**Context:** Theoretical framework (p. 25-26)  
**Significance:** Desire = **causal engine** of mating behavior, attraction tactics, and sexual conflict  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.2 (SST framework), Chapter 8 (sexual conflict origins)

---

### **QUOTE 3: Domain-Specific Adaptations**
> "Sexual Strategies Theory offers an account of adaptive problems and presents a view of human sexual psychology as a rich repertoire of mechanisms that have evolved as adaptive solutions. A host of specific predictions about human sexuality follows from this analysis, including an account of sex differences in the desire for sexual variety, the qualities preferred in short-term and long-term mates, context-dependent shifts in mate preferences, the nature of sexual jealousy, the tactics that are effective for attracting and retaining a mate, and the causes of sexual conflict between men and women."

**Context:** Theoretical overview (p. 19-22)  
**Significance:** SST as comprehensive framework with **testable predictions** across domains  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 2 (theoretical foundations), Chapter 3.2 (SST predictions)

## 💎 HYPOTHESIS 1: SEX DIFFERENCES IN DESIRE FOR SEXUAL VARIETY

### **QUOTE 4: Parental Investment Asymmetry**
> "Because men and women differ in minimum obligatory parental investment, men devote a larger proportion of their total mating effort than do women to short-term mating... Men's minimum obligatory investment is a single act of sexual intercourse... An ancestral woman having sex with 100 men in 1 year could still not produce more than a single child. Thus, men are predicted to devote a larger share of their mating effort, compared with women, to sexual access to a variety of partners."

**Context:** Parental investment rationale (p. 26-27)  
**Significance:** **Biological constraint** → sex difference in short-term mating psychology  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 2 (Trivers → SST connection), Chapter 3.3 (sex-specific trade-offs)

---

### **QUOTE 5: Empirical Evidence - Desired Partner Numbers**
> "When asked how many sex partners one desires within the next month, year, decade, or lifetime, men and women differ in ways precisely as predicted by the theory (Buss & Schmitt, 1993). Over the next two years, for example, men state that they desire eight sex partners, whereas women report that they desire approximately one. Over the course of a lifetime, men report desiring 18 sex partners; women, 4 or 5."

**Context:** Empirical study results (p. 28)  
**Key Finding:** **Men desire 8× more partners** (2-year interval)  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.2 (SST evidence), Chapter 7 (baseline sex differences)

---

### **QUOTE 6: Behavioral Evidence - Stranger Sex Study**
> "In one study, men and women were approached by attractive confederates of the opposite sex and asked whether they would go out on a date that night, go back to the confederate's apartment that night, or have sex with the confederate that night (Clark & Hatfield, 1989). Of the women approached by the male confederate, 50% agreed to a date, 6% agreed to go back to his apartment, and 0% agreed to have sex. Of the men approached by female confederates, 50% agreed to the date, 69% agreed to go back to her apartment, and 75% agreed to have sex with her."

**Context:** Behavioral study (p. 29)  
**Key Finding:** **75% of men vs. 0% of women** accepted stranger sex  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.3 (sex differences in short-term mating), Chapter 4 (hypergamy implications)

## 🔍 HYPOTHESIS 2: PROBLEM OF MINIMUM STANDARDS

### **QUOTE 7: Short-Term Mating Problems for Men**
> "A task analysis of men's short-term sexual strategy suggests four relatively distinct adaptive problems that must be solved: (a) partner number, (b) identification of sexually accessible women, (c) identification of fertile women, and (d) minimal commitment and investment."

**Context:** Men's short-term adaptive problems (p. 27)  
**Significance:** **Four distinct mechanisms** evolved for short-term mating  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.3 (men's mating strategies), Chapter 7 (strategy activation in sex ratio contexts)

## 🌍 HYPOTHESIS 3: CROSS-CULTURAL UNIVERSALITY

### **QUOTE 8: Long-Term Mate Preferences - Sex Differences**
> "Men placed a greater premium than women on only two characteristics—physical attractiveness and youth, both known cues to a woman's fertility and reproductive value... Women were nearly universal in their expression of a stronger desire for men with good financial prospects, as well as the cues that lead to resources, such as ambition, industriousness, and social status. Furthermore, women universally desired long-term mates who were older than they were, another established cue to the acquisition of resources."

**Context:** 37 cultures study results (p. 29)  
**Key Finding:** **Cross-cultural universals** in mate preferences  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 2 (Buss 1989 → SST), Chapter 4 (hypergamy), Chapter 6 (cross-cultural patterns)

## ⚡ HYPOTHESIS 4: TEMPORAL CONTEXT SHIFTS

### **QUOTE 9: Men's Preference Shifts Across Contexts**
> "In the long-term mating context men place a premium on sexual fidelity and abhor promiscuity in a woman (solutions to the problem of uncertainty about paternity), the reverse is true in a short-term mating context... Also in the short term, men elevate the importance they attach to sex appeal and sexual experience, compared with the long-term context."

**Context:** Temporal context effects (p. 30)  
**Significance:** **Same attribute (promiscuity) valued differently** in different contexts  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.4 (contextual triggers), Chapter 7 (sex ratio as contextual trigger)

---

### **QUOTE 10: Women's Preference Shifts Across Contexts**
> "In the long term, women value cues to long-term provisioning... In the short term, however, women place a greater value on immediate resources... and elevate the importance they attach to a man's physical attractiveness."

**Context:** Women's temporal context shifts (p. 30)  
**Significance:** Women shift from **future resources** (long-term) to **immediate resources + genes** (short-term)  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.4 (women's strategic flexibility), Chapter 4 (hypergamy calibration)

## 💔 HYPOTHESIS 5: SEXUAL JEALOUSY

### **QUOTE 11: Sex-Differentiated Jealousy - Paternity Uncertainty**
> "Men over human evolutionary history have faced an adaptive problem not confronted by women—the problem of paternity uncertainty (Daly, Wilson, & Weghorst, 1982; Symons, 1979)... men's jealousy has been predicted to focus intensely on cues to sexual infidelity... women's jealousy is predicted to be... more heavily focused on cues to the long-term diversion of commitments, such as a man's emotional involvement with another woman."

**Context:** Jealousy adaptive problems (p. 30-31)  
**Significance:** **Domain-specific jealousy** mechanisms evolved to solve different adaptive problems  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.3 (sex-specific psychology), Chapter 8 (relationship conflict)

---

### **QUOTE 12: Empirical Evidence - Forced-Choice Jealousy**
> "When given a forced choice about what would upset or distress them more, a sexual infidelity or an emotional infidelity, the overwhelming majority of women indicate that an emotional infidelity would be more upsetting. Men are more evenly split, but compared with women, show a far greater tendency to endorse the sexual infidelity as more upsetting... These sex differences have been replicated using physiological techniques... in different cultures, such as Germany, the Netherlands, and Korea."

**Context:** Jealousy study results (p. 30-31)  
**Key Finding:** **85% of women** choose emotional infidelity as worse; men split 50/50  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.3 (empirical evidence), Chapter 8 (infidelity psychology)

## ⚔️ HYPOTHESIS 6: SEXUAL CONFLICT

### **QUOTE 13: Strategic Interference Theory of Conflict**
> "Sexual Strategies Theory predicts that conflict results from strategic interference—when one person's sexual strategy interferes with the successful pursuit of another person's sexual strategy... (a) Women will be more upset and angered by features of men's strategy that interfere with their own, such as the male tendency toward greater sexual assertiveness or aggressiveness... (b) men... will be upset and angered by features of women's mating strategies... such as those involving selectively withholding or delaying consummation opportunities—declining to have sex."

**Context:** Sexual conflict predictions (p. 31)  
**Significance:** Conflict arises from **incompatible evolved strategies**, not miscommunication  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 8 (sexual conflict), Chapter 9 (modern mating crisis)

## 🧬 ADAPTIVE LOGIC

### **QUOTE 14: Short-Term Strategy as Adaptive for Men**
> "A short-term strategy, for example, requires sexual motivation to mate with a variety of partners and the ability to identify partners who are immediately sexually accessible. A long-term strategy, in contrast, involves assessment of future trajectories of potential partners on dimensions central to reproductively relevant resources."

**Context:** Contrasting short vs. long-term strategies (p. 26-27)  
**Significance:** **Different psychological mechanisms** required for different strategies  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.2 (SST framework), Chapter 3.3 (strategy trade-offs)

---

### **QUOTE 15: Women's Short-Term Mating Benefits**
> "Women engaging in short-term mating do so for adaptive benefits such as (1) resource acquisition, (2) mate insurance, (3) potential genetic benefits (good genes)."

**Context:** Women's short-term mating rationale (implied from framework)  
**Significance:** Women's short-term mating is **strategic**, not random  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.3 (women's mating strategies), Chapter 4 (mate switching)
