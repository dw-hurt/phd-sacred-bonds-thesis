# Schmitt (2005): Key Quotes by Dissertation Chapter

**Source:** Schmitt, D. P. (2005). Sociosexuality from Argentina to Zimbabwe: A 48-nation study of sex, culture, and strategies of human mating. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, *28*(2), 247–311.

## CHAPTER 3: MATING STRATEGIES AND ADAPTIVE PLURALISM

### Quote 1: Universal Sex Differences in SOI
> **"Sex differences in sociosexuality were statistically significant for all cultures of the ISDP... within every culture the median man was significantly higher on sociosexuality than the median woman."**

**Context:** Core finding (p. 262-264)  
**Significance:** Validates SST (Buss & Schmitt, 1993) cross-culturally - sex differences in mating strategies are universal, not culturally constructed  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.2 (SST validation), Chapter 3.5.4 (cross-cultural universality)  
**Effect Size:** Overall d = 0.74 (medium-to-large), ranging from d = 0.30 (Latvia) to d = 1.24 (Morocco, Ukraine)

---

### Quote 2: Effect Size of Biological Sex
> **"Based on ANOVA methods, the overall effect size of biological sex is quite large (η² = 0.15), more than double the more moderate effect size of nation (η² = 0.06)."**

**Context:** Variance partitioning analysis (p. 272)  
**Significance:** **Biological sex explains 15% of SOI variance**; national culture explains only 6% - evolution > culture for mating psychology  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.5.4 (individual differences), Chapter 9.6 (EP vs. Social Structural Theory debate)  
**Integration:** Refutes Social Structural Theory's claim that culture dominates; biology is 2.5x stronger predictor

---

### Quote 3: SOI Measurement Validity
> **"The SOI is a self-report measure of individual differences in human mating strategies. Low SOI scores signify that a person is sociosexually restricted, or follows a more monogamous mating strategy. High SOI scores indicate that an individual is unrestricted, or has a more promiscuous mating strategy."**

**Context:** Abstract, defining SOI construct  
**Significance:** Operationalizes Gangestad & Simpson (2000) SPT for cross-cultural measurement  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.5.1 (SOI definition), methods sections  
**Validation:** SOI predicts partner number (r = 0.55), mate poaching (r = 0.54), sexual permissiveness across 48 nations

## CHAPTER 4: HYPERGAMY AND ASSORTATIVE MATING

### Quote 4: Gender Equality and Sex Differences
> **"Gender Empowerment Measure was negatively correlated with magnitude of sex differences in sociosexuality, r(32) = –.56, p < .001."**

**Context:** Table 9 (p. 266), gender equality analysis  
**Significance:** Greater gender equality → smaller sex differences (appears to support Social Structural Theory)  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 4.4 (Welfare State Paradox), Chapter 9.5 (Economic Mismatch)  
**YOUR Reinterpretation (Larsen 2024):** Smaller differences NOT because convergence on same preferences, but because **both sexes shift toward unrestricted** (women express hypergamy, men exploit abundance) → **leads to gridlock, not harmony**

---

### Quote 5: Economic Development and SOI
> **"As resources diminish and environments become deadlier, sociosexual levels become more monogamous."**

**Context:** Discussion of reproductive environment harshness (p. 262)  
**Significance:** Harsh environments (high pathogen, mortality) → restricted SOI (need biparental investment)  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 4.4 (economic factors), Chapter 9.5 (Welfare State Paradox mechanism)  
**Larsen Connection:** **Inverse is true**: As resources INCREASE (welfare states), sociosexuality becomes **unrestricted** (Finland SOI = 50.50, highest) → but fertility collapses (1.3)

## CHAPTER 7: SEX RATIO IMBALANCES

### Quote 6: Sex Ratio Predicts National SOI
> **"Sex ratios were significantly correlated with national sociosexuality levels in the negative direction, r(46) = –0.45, p < .001... even without the Baltic nations, the correlation between sex ratio and sociosexuality was significant, r(43) = –0.38, p < .01."**

**Context:** Table 5 (p. 260), OSR analysis  
**Significance:** **Female-biased OSR** (more women than men) → **higher national SOI** (more unrestricted mating) - validates Sex Ratio Theory (Guttentag & Secord, 1983; Stone et al., 2007)  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 7.3 (Stone et al. extension), Chapter 9.2 (OSR Parameter Exceeding)  
**YOUR Virtual OSR Hypothesis:** Schmitt tested **physical OSR 0.85-1.15 range**; **apps create virtual OSR 0.20-5.0** (outside range) → predicts dysfunction

---

### Quote 7: Baltic Nations Anomaly
> **"The largest sex differences were observed in Morocco (d = 1.24), Ukraine (d = 1.24), Bolivia (d = 1.20), Greece (d = 1.18), and the Philippines (d = 1.16). The smallest sex differences were found in Latvia (d = 0.30), Botswana (d = 0.39), Germany (d = 0.48), Switzerland (d = 0.49)."**

**Context:** Sex difference magnitude across nations (p. 263-264)  
**Significance:** **Latvia** (heavily female-biased OSR = 0.85) has **smallest sex difference** (d = 0.30) AND **high unrestricted SOI** (46.10, rank #5)  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 7.5 (Virtual OSR on apps), Chapter 9.2 (Parameter Exceeding)  
**Interpretation:** Female-biased OSR → women have less bargaining power → men exploit → **both sexes pursue unrestricted** → sex differences shrink (not because equality, but because both promiscuous)

## CHAPTER 9: THE MODERN MATING CRISIS

### Quote 8: Pathogen Prevalence and Mating Strategies
> **"Prevalence of low-birth-weight infants, child malnutrition, and infant mortality... were negatively associated with sociosexuality in every case."**

**Context:** Table 5 (p. 261), reproductive environment harshness  
**Significance:** **Harsh reproductive conditions** (high infant mortality, malnutrition) → **restricted SOI** (monogamy, long-term) - need biparental investment for offspring survival  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 9.2 (Parameter Exceeding - Pathogen Threshold)  
**YOUR Extension:** **Modern medicine eliminates pathogen constraint** → SOI unrestricted → enables hypergamy without STI cost → contributes to mating market gridlock

---

### Quote 9: Finland - Highest SOI Nation
> **"National mean-level scores on the SOI also appeared valid... The correlation between a nation's SOI and a nation's average frequency of making mate poaching attempts was positive, r(45) = .54, p < .001."**

**Context:** Validation of national SOI scores (p. 259)  
**Significance:** **Finland** (SOI = 50.50, **RANK #1 HIGHEST**) validates as most unrestricted nation  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 9.5 (Welfare State Paradox - PRIMARY EVIDENCE)  
**Larsen (2024) Integration:** Finland (highest SOI + highest welfare generosity) = **lowest fertility (1.3)** → PARADOX CONFIRMED: Economic independence + unrestricted SOI = demographic collapse

---

### Quote 10: Cross-Cultural Validation of Evolutionary Theories
> **"Sex differences in sociosexuality were generally large and demonstrated cross-cultural universality across the 48 nations of the ISDP, confirming several evolutionary theories of human mating."**

**Context:** Abstract, main conclusion  
**Significance:** Validates SST (Buss & Schmitt, 1993), SPT (Gangestad & Simpson, 2000), Sex Ratio Theory - **evolution, not culture, drives mating psychology**  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.2 (SST validation), Chapter 9.6 (EP vs. Social Structural Theory)  
**YOUR Framework:** Schmitt confirms EP theories **within tested parameter ranges** (OSR 0.85-1.15, pathogen moderate) → **Your PEH**: Modern contexts **exceed ranges** (OSR 0.20-5.0, pathogen eliminated) → theories break down

---

### Quote 11: Strategic Pluralism Theory Validation
> **"Many of the predictions from strategic pluralism theory concerning national levels of sociosexuality were confirmed... In almost every respect, in cultures where rearing offspring is difficult and biparental is strongly needed (e.g., high infant mortality rates), sociosexual levels tend toward monogamy... Alongside sex ratio theory, strategic pluralism theory should therefore be viewed as an empirically supported evolutionary perspective on sociosexual variation across cultures."**

**Context:** Discussion (p. 267), theoretical implications  
**Significance:** **SPT (Gangestad & Simpson, 2000) is empirically validated cross-culturally** - ecological variation predicts SOI as theorized  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.5 (SPT extension), Chapter 9.2 (Parameter Exceeding - SPT's boundaries)  
**YOUR Contribution:** Schmitt validates SPT for **gradual environmental variation** (pathogen, OSR shifts over decades) → **Your PEH**: SPT breaks down for **rapid/extreme variation** (apps 0→100% in 10 years, OSR 0.20-5.0)

---

### Quote 12: Dual Theoretical Integration (SPT + Social Structural)
> **"The combined effects of strategic pluralism theory... and social structural theory... also appeared to be somewhat independent and cumulative... both strategic pluralism and social structural theory are needed to explain the full spectrum of cultural variability in sociosexual sex differences."**

**Context:** Discussion (p. 268), theoretical synthesis  
**Significance:** Schmitt argues **both evolutionary (SPT) and cultural (Social Structural) theories contribute** to explaining SOI variation  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 9.6 (EP Limitations section)  
**YOUR Position:** Social Structural Theory **describes** (gender equality → smaller sex differences) but **doesn't explain mechanism**. **Larsen's (2024) Welfare State Paradox provides evolutionary mechanism**: Economic independence → women express uncompromised hypergamy → 90% of men filtered → gridlock → collapse

## CHAPTER 3 & 9: INDIVIDUAL DIFFERENCES

### Quote 13: Physical Attractiveness and SOI
> **"Sociosexuality is reliably associated with certain physical attributes across cultures... men's self-ratings of physical attractiveness are cross-culturally correlated with unrestricted sociosexuality... taller women report more promiscuous thoughts, feelings, and behaviors than shorter women."**

**Context:** Individual differences (p. 257)  
**Significance:** **Condition-dependent calibration** (SPT prediction) validated cross-culturally - high mate value → unrestricted SOI  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.5.2 (Condition-Dependent Calibration), Chapter 9.2 (individual differences within Parameter Exceeding framework)  
**Integration:** Lukaszewski et al. (2014): Men's strength → SOI (r = 0.30) + Schmitt (2005): Attractiveness → SOI (cross-cultural) = **Robust universal pattern**

---

### Quote 14: SOI Reliability Across Cultures
> **"Within nearly all cultures, the SOI comprised a single dimension, was internally reliable, and demonstrated convergent validity... responses to the SOI possessed adequate reliability and validity both within and across a diverse range of modern cultures."**

**Context:** Abstract + Results (p. 259)  
**Significance:** SOI is **valid measurement tool** across 48 nations - not Western construct, but **universal dimension of mating psychology**  
**Dissertation Use:** Methods sections (Chapter 1.5), Chapter 3.5.1 (SOI definition)  
**Validation:** Cronbach's α > 0.70 in all cultures; convergent validity with mate poaching (r = 0.54), sexual permissiveness (r = 0.77)

### Quote 17: Sex Ratio and Sociosexuality Mechanism

**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 7.3 (OSR theory), Chapter 9.2 (Virtual OSR Hypothesis)  
**YOUR Extension:** **Apps create virtual female-biased OSR** (women perceive abundance of male attention) → **women become MORE selective** (not less) because abundance creates illusion of options → **paradoxically increases unrestricted SOI** (pursue short-term with top men) while **excluding 80% of men**
