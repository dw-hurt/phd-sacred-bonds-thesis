## Chapter 2: Literature Review (FOUNDATIONAL)

**Quote 1:** "Parental investment is defined as any investment by the parent in an individual offspring that increases the offspring's chance of surviving at the cost of the parent's ability to invest in other offspring."
- **Integration:** Chapter 2.1 - Core Definitions

**Quote 2:** "What governs the operation of sexual selection is the relative parental investment of the sexes in their offspring."
- **Integration:** Chapter 2.2 - Sexual Selection Theory

**Quote 3:** "Where one sex invests considerably more than the other, members of the latter will compete among themselves to mate with members of the former."
- **Integration:** Chapter 2.2, 7.4 - Competition Patterns

---

## Chapter 3: Mating Strategies

**Quote 4:** "Since female reproductive success is limited more by ability to produce and raise offspring than by ability to locate mates, females should be choosier than males."
- **Integration:** Chapter 3.7 - Female Mating Strategies

**Quote 5:** "Since male reproductive success is usually limited more by access to females than by ability to produce gametes, males typically compete more intensely for mates."
- **Integration:** Chapter 3.6 - Male Mating Strategies

## Chapter 4: Economic Dimensions

**Quote 6:** "The female's minimum investment is much greater than the male's. She contributes the egg, and in mammals she also gestates and lactates. The male need only contribute a single sperm."
- **Integration:** Chapter 4.2 - Investment Asymmetry & Hypergamy

## Chapter 7: Sex Ratio Dynamics

**Quote 7:** "In species where sex ratio is biased, the more numerous sex should be less discriminating in mate choice, as competition for mates is more intense."
- **Integration:** Chapter 7.3 - Sex Ratio Theory Foundation

## Chapter 6: Cultural Evolution

**Quote 8:** "Sex role reversal occurs in species where males invest more than females, such as pipefish where males carry offspring. This demonstrates that investment patterns, not chromosomal sex, determine mating strategies."
- **Integration:** Chapter 6.3 - Context-Dependence of Sex Roles
