## CHAPTER 2: LITERATURE REVIEW (Evolutionary Psychology Foundations)

### Quote 1: The Iron Law of Evolution
> **"Mating is the foundation of every society. Unless a community is able to get their mating to work, that society will disappear. That is an iron law of evolution."**

**Context:** Opening argument (02:10)  
**Significance:** Frames mating crisis as **existential demographic threat**, not just individual suffering.  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 2.7 (Evolutionary Mismatch Introduction), Chapter 9.1 (Crisis Introduction)

### Quote 2: The Dual Mating System
> **"Humans have two evolved mating systems: promiscuous mating (seeking good genes/attraction) and pair-bonding (seeking support/resources). In ancestral environments, women used BOTH strategies but with DIFFERENT men. Modern 'free market' mating collapses this distinction—women now expect BOTH traits in ONE man, which only ~5% of men possess."**

**Context:** Core theoretical framework (1:07:00)  
**Significance:** Explains **why** modern mating produces winner-take-all dynamics.  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.5 (SPT extension), Chapter 9.2 (Parameter Exceeding Hypothesis)  
**Integration:** Extends Gangestad & Simpson (2000) SPT, explains Larsen (2023) Tinder inequality.

## CHAPTER 3: MATING STRATEGIES AND ADAPTIVE PLURALISM

### Quote 3: The First Sexual Revolution (12th Century Monogamy)
> **"The Catholic Church's enforcement of lifelong monogamy in the 12th century was the most consequential social engineering project in human history. It broke kinship groups, forced powerful men to release women, and gave average men access to the mating market for the first time. This created Western prosperity."**

**Context:** Historical analysis (33:00)  
**Significance:** **Proves cultural interventions CAN override evolved hypergamy**—critical for policy proposals (Chapter 10).  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 2.3 (Cultural Evolution), Chapter 9.4 (Historical Precedents), Chapter 10.2 (Policy Feasibility)  
**Integration:** Shows Trivers (1972) parental investment can be **socially manipulated**, not just biologically determined.

---

### Quote 4: Modern Mating as Unprecedented Experiment
> **"Modern mating is the result of the first time in human history women have been free to choose their own partners on a mass scale... and we are not handling it."**

**Context:** Third Sexual Revolution analysis (45:00)  
**Significance:** Positions modern mating crisis as **novel evolutionary challenge**, not just extension of ancestral patterns.  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.6 (Modern Contexts), Chapter 9.1 (Crisis Introduction)  
**Integration:** Contrasts with Buss & Schmitt (1993) SST assumption that strategies are **context-independent**.

## CHAPTER 4: HYPERGAMY AND ASSORTATIVE MATING

### Quote 5: The Welfare State Paradox
> **"In social democracies like Norway, women receive .2 million more in lifetime benefits than they pay in taxes. Men are net tax contributors. The state has effectively replaced the husband as provider. This eliminates women's economic incentive to pair-bond with average men."**

**Context:** Economic analysis (1:08:00)  
**Significance:** **Revolutionary claim**: Welfare states inadvertently destroyed mating markets by making male provisioning obsolete.  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 4.4 (Economic Hypergamy), Chapter 9.5 (Economic Mismatch)  
**Integration:** Explains **why** Bertrand et al. (2015) findings (female income → divorce) are amplified in social democracies.

---

### Quote 6: Hypergamy Unleashed
> **"Women aren't being unreasonable—their evolved preferences are responding accurately to signals. The problem is modern signals (Tinder photos, wealth displays) create the illusion that top-tier men are accessible, when historically only ~5% of women could pair with such men via polygyny."**

**Context:** Mate preference analysis (1:07:00)  
**Significance:** Shifts blame from **individual women** to **systemic design** (critical for avoiding misogyny in dissertation).  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 4.3 (Mate Preferences), Chapter 7.5 (Virtual OSR), Chapter 9.3 (Signal Reliability Collapse)  
**Integration:** Reconciles Buss (1989) universal preferences with Larsen (2023) inequality—preferences are **constant**, **contexts** changed.

## CHAPTER 7: SEX RATIO IMBALANCES AND RELATIONAL DYNAMICS

### Quote 7: The 30,000 Swipe Problem
> **"For a median man on Tinder, some studies suggest he has to work through 30,000 profiles before he gets a mating opportunity."**

**Context:** Dating app dysfunction (1:01:00)  
**Significance:** **Quantifies** the parameter exceeding hypothesis—ancestral mating pool = 50-150, modern = 30,000.  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 7.5 (Virtual OSR on Apps), Chapter 9.2 (Mating Pool Size Mismatch)  
**Integration:** Shows Stone et al. (2007) OSR effects (state-level, 0.90-1.10 range) **cannot predict** app-level dynamics (effective OSR = 0.20 for avg men).

---

### Quote 8: Female Filtration Cascade
> **"Women delete 95% of men based on looks, then filter another 98% of the remainder after chatting, then 80% after meeting. This leaves 0.01% of the original male pool deemed 'acceptable.'"**

**Context:** App behavior analysis (59:00)  
**Significance:** **Operationalizes hypergamy** at scale—shows it's not "women want top 20%," it's "women filter to top 0.01%."  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 7.5 (App Dynamics), Chapter 9.2 (Parameter Exceeding)  
**Integration:** Explains **why** Gangestad & Simpson (2000) condition-dependent calibration **fails**—modern contexts exceed calibration range.

---

### Quote 9: Virtual vs. Physical Sex Ratios
> **"Dating apps create a male-biased physical sex ratio (76% male users on Tinder), but because women match only with the top 5-10% of men, the EFFECTIVE sex ratio for average men is 0.2—twenty men competing per available woman."**

**Context:** OSR analysis (implicit throughout interview, synthesized from 59:00 + 1:01:00)  
**Significance:** Distinguishes **physical OSR** (demographic reality) from **virtual OSR** (behavioral reality).  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 7.3 (OSR Theory Extension), Chapter 7.5 (Virtual OSR), Chapter 9.2 (Threshold Effects)  
**Integration:** **Revolutionary extension** of Stone et al. (2007)—OSR theory must account for **attention-based** ratios, not just population ratios.

## CHAPTER 8: RELATIONSHIP DYNAMICS AND CONFLICT

### Quote 10: The Dual Dysfunction (Incels and Insings)
> **"Women feel they are only good enough for sex, not relationships. Men feel they aren't good enough for anything."**

**Context:** Relationship outcomes (1:02:00)  
**Significance:** Captures the **symmetry** of modern mating dysfunction—**both sexes suffer**, but differently.  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 8.4 (Mating Market Dysfunction), Chapter 9.3 (Cascading Failures)  
**Integration:** Explains Buss & Schmitt (1993) sexual conflict in modern terms—conflict now stems from **market structure**, not just strategic interference.

### Quote 11: Why Top Men Don't Commit
> **"On dating apps, high-value men face no scarcity. Why would they commit when unlimited sexual access is available? Ancestrally, even high-status men faced constraints (limited female availability, kin group pressure). Apps remove all constraints."**

**Context:** Male strategic recalibration (1:05:00)  
**Significance:** Explains **why** Buss & Schmitt (1993) prediction (men pursue long-term when competition is high) **fails** on apps.  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 3.4 (Strategic Flexibility Limits), Chapter 8.3 (Commitment Dynamics)  
**Integration:** Shows SST assumes **scarcity**, but apps create **abundance** for top-tier men, changing strategic calculus.

### Quote 14: Why SPT Fails in Extreme Contexts
> **"Strategic Pluralism Theory assumes environments change gradually, allowing facultative recalibration. But dating apps emerged in 10 years—from zero to total market dominance. Evolution cannot calibrate to changes this rapid."**

**Context:** Theoretical critique (implicit throughout, synthesized from 1:05:00 + 1:07:00)  
**Significance:** **First explicit critique** of SPT's core assumption (gradual environmental change).  
**Dissertation Use:** Chapter 9.2 (Threshold Effects Hypothesis), Chapter 9.6 (EP Limitations)  
**Integration:** Your dissertation's **revolutionary contribution**—identifying **temporal mismatches** (rapid tech change) as distinct from **parametric mismatches** (extreme values).
