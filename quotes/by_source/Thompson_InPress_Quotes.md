### Chapter 1: Introduction - Contemporary Mating Crisis

#### Quote 1: Digital Dating Prevalence ⭐⭐⭐
**Quote:** "With the invention of the smart phone and mobile apps, online dating has become one of the most common means of connecting (Shepherd, 2016). In the U.S. alone, approximately 33.9 million users accessed online dating services in 2018 and the number is expected to reach 37.2 million by 2022 (Kunst 2019)." (p.2)

**Theme:** Technology impact, mating market dynamics  
**Priority:** Critical - establishes scope of digital transformation  
**Usage Context:** Introduce scale of contemporary shift to digital mate selection in Chapter 1  
**Integration Note:** Pair with evolutionary mismatch arguments about modern vs. ancestral mating environments

#### Quote 2: Mononormativity as Dominant Script ⭐⭐⭐
**Quote:** "Since mononormativity, the belief system that 'establishes the monogamous (and heterosexual) couple as natural, optimal, and morally loftier' (Ferrer 2018: 819)—is the dominant script that underpins ideals of romantic love and intimate relations in our society (Wolkomir 2019), for the millions of people who ascribe to non-monogamy, online profile creation is often complicated by dating platform interfaces and relationship structures." (p.3)

**Theme:** Relationship norms, cultural expectations  
**Priority:** Critical - defines key theoretical concept  
**Usage Context:** Establish cultural context for contemporary mating norms

#### Quote 3: Cross-Cultural Monogamy Data ⭐⭐⭐
**Quote:** "Monogamy has been shown to be less common than non-monogamy, with strictly monogamous cultures accounting for only 17% of human cultures (Chapais 2013). A representative study in the U.S. found that '21% of the study's participants reported having had some kind of non-monogamous relationship— which the study defined as "any relationship in which all partners agree that each may have romantic and/or sexual relationships with other partners"' (Haupert et al. 2017). Rubel & Burleigh (2020) estimate that at least 1.44 million adults in the U.S. would fall within the category of polyamorous." (p.6)

**Theme:** Cross-cultural patterns, prevalence data  
**Priority:** Critical - challenges monogamy-as-universal assumption  
**Usage Context:** Evolutionary foundations section; shows mating strategy diversity  
**Integration Note:** Supports thesis that human mating is flexible, not rigidly monogamous

#### Quote 4: The Relationship Escalator ⭐⭐
**Quote:** "Traditional notions of how people 'should' do love and sexual relationships are rooted in ideals of heterosexual monogamy, characterized by a man and woman falling in love, committing to an exclusive emotional and sexual pair-bonded relationship, and living happily ever after. Gahran (2012) uses the term 'relationship escalator' to describe these ideals as unquestioned expectations about how intimate relationships between two people (and only two people) progress..." (p.4)

**Theme:** Cultural scripts, normative expectations  
**Priority:** Important - conceptualizes relationship progression script  
**Usage Context:** Introduce concept of culturally scripted mating patterns

#### Quote 9: Visibility Creates Acceptance ⭐⭐⭐
**Quote:** "... when a platform does not include an option on a field form to identify one's relationship style the normative style is automatically assumed as the default. By including non-monogamy as an option, monogamy is no longer assumed as the default (and only option). As pointed out by OkCupid co-founder Christian Rudder (2014: 188), 'when a large portion of a group goes unrecognized, it only makes marginalizing the whole easier. Visibility, on the other hand, creates acceptance.'" (p.14)

**Theme:** Platform design, normativity, visibility  
**Priority:** Critical - core thesis about design impact  
**Usage Context:** Analyze how interface shapes social norms  
**Integration Note:** Connect to synchronicity (visibility creates legitimacy field)

---

#### Quote 10: Platform Evolution and Inclusive Design ⭐⭐
**Quote:** "OkCupid began to integrate inclusive design changes aimed at sexually marginalized users in response to a boycott (Berg 2014) and petition that critiqued the limits of the 'rigid gender and sexuality options' (Pogensky 2013). Shortly after, OkCupid developers added thirteen sexual orientation options and twenty-two gender options (Wong 2014) followed by the addition of relationship orientation options in 2016. Adding non-traditional relationship styles on a mainstream dating platform has the potential to contribute to changing perceptions of what is 'normal' merely by making them visible through the integration of labels and menus that name them." (p.13-14)

**Theme:** Platform evolution, norm disruption  
**Priority:** Important - documents progressive changes  
**Usage Context:** Trace evolution of platform inclusivity  
**Integration Note:** Discuss tension between commercial and social justice motives

---

#### Quote 11: Design Reinforces Mononormativity (Main Thesis) ⭐⭐⭐
**Quote:** "I argue that while the addition of non-monogamy features on OkCupid appear to challenge mononormativity on the surface, the platform design and user discursive practices simultaneously reinscribe hegemonic coupledom as the preferred way to do relationships. When dating platforms like OkCupid employ semiotic resources that emphasize pair-bonding—digitally, visually, and/or linguistically—they reinforce mononormativity." (p.35)

**Theme:** Persistence of norms, subtle reinforcement  
**Priority:** Critical - Thompson's central argument  
**Usage Context:** Critical analysis of platform limitations  
**Integration Note:** Use to argue technology reflects/reinforces existing power structures

## USAGE GUIDELINES

### Priority Levels
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Critical:** Essential quotes that directly support main thesis arguments
- ⭐⭐ **Important:** Strong supporting evidence for key points
- ⭐ **Supplementary:** Additional context or future directions

### Integration Best Practices

1. **Always Provide Context:** Don't drop quotes without framing
   - WEAK: "As Thompson notes, 'mononormativity' is the dominant script (p.3)."
   - STRONG: "Digital dating platforms, despite expanding options, still encode what Thompson (in press) terms 'mononormativity,' the belief system that 'establishes the monogamous (and heterosexual) couple as natural, optimal, and morally loftier' (p.3)."

2. **Connect to Your Framework:** Show how quote supports Sacred Bonds dimensions
   - Example: "This visibility paradox reflects synchronistic tensions: while naming creates legitimacy fields (Jung), incomplete representation maintains collective unconscious patterns privileging coupledom."

3. **Combine with Other Sources:** Cross-reference with other literature
   - Example: "Thompson's finding that only 17% of cultures are strictly monogamous (p.6) aligns with Gangestad & Simpson's (2000) evolutionary perspective on human mating strategy diversity."

4. **Use for Multiple Purposes:** Same quote can support different arguments in different chapters
   - Chapter 1: Use prevalence data to establish scope of crisis
   - Chapter 4: Use same data to justify focus on digital platforms
   - Chapter 7: Use to legitimize CNM as research focus

## COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS CONNECTIONS

### Supports Firman & Larsen
- Both emphasize biological/evolutionary foundations of mating strategy diversity
- Thompson provides cultural/technological manifestation of evolutionary flexibility Firman & Larsen describe

### Extends Gangestad & Simpson (2000)
- G&S focus on individual differences in mating strategies
- Thompson shows how technological platforms constrain which strategies are viable in practice

### Complements Buss (2023)
- Buss emphasizes evolutionary roots of mate preferences
- Thompson demonstrates how digital platforms mediate (and distort) preference expression

### Contrasts with Bertrand et al.
- Bertrand focuses on economic/demographic factors in mating markets
- Thompson highlights cultural/technological factors, showing crisis is multi-causal

### Supports Larsen (2023)
- Both critique technological solutions as insufficient
- Thompson provides empirical evidence for Larsen's theoretical concerns about digital mediation
