## Chapter 2: Literature Review

**Quote 1:** "These findings provide strong support for evolutionary hypotheses about mate selection. The cross-cultural consistency suggests that mate preferences reflect evolved psychological mechanisms rather than arbitrary cultural constructions."

## Chapter 3: Mating Strategies

**Quote 2:** "Women in all 37 cultures preferred mates who were older than themselves (mean = 3.42 years older), while men in all 37 cultures preferred mates who were younger (mean = 2.66 years younger)."
- **Integration:** Chapter 3.3 - Age Preferences & Fertility

**Quote 3:** "Men valued physical attractiveness in potential mates significantly more than women did across all 37 cultures (d = -0.81)."
- **Integration:** Chapter 3.6 - Male Mate Preferences

## Chapter 4: Economic Dimensions

**Quote 4:** "In 36 of 37 cultures, women valued good financial prospects in a mate significantly more than men did, yielding an effect size of d = 0.90. This represents one of the most robust sex differences ever documented."
- **Integration:** Chapter 4.2 - Hypergamy Empirical Foundation

**Quote 5:** "Women valued ambition and industriousness significantly more than men in 29 of 37 cultures (d = 0.67), consistent with the hypothesis that women seek cues to long-term resource acquisition potential."
- **Integration:** Chapter 4.3 - Future Earnings Preferences

**Quote 6:** "Women's preference for older mates likely reflects the fact that age correlates with resource accumulation and social status in most societies."
- **Integration:** Chapter 4.4 - Age as Resource Proxy

---

## Chapter 6: Cultural Evolution

**Quote 7:** "Preferences for chastity showed substantial cultural variation, with high valuation in China, India, and Iran, but near-zero valuation in Sweden and the Netherlands."
- **Integration:** Chapter 6.3 - Cultural Modulation of Universal Psychology

**Quote 8:** "While the direction of sex differences was universal, the magnitude varied across cultures, suggesting cultural amplification or suppression of evolved preferences."
- **Integration:** Chapter 6.4 - Evoked vs. Transmitted Culture

---

## Chapter 7: Sex Ratio Dynamics

**Quote 9:** "The consistency of mate preferences across diverse cultures with varying sex ratios suggests that preferences have deep evolutionary roots, though their expression may be modulated by local demographic conditions."
- **Integration:** Chapter 7.1 - Baseline Preferences vs. Contextual Adjustment

---

## Chapter 8: Synthesis

**Quote 10:** "The combination of universal patterns and cultural variation suggests that human mate preferences result from the interaction of evolved psychology and local ecology."
- **Integration:** Chapter 8.2 - Gene-Culture Coevolution
