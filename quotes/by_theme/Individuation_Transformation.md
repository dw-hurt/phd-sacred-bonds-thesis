## Key Concepts

### 1. Jung's Individuation Process
The lifelong psychological development toward wholeness, integration, and realization of one's unique potential.

**Relevance to Pair Bonding**:
- Intimate relationship as primary context for individuation
- Partner relationship challenges prompt psychological growth
- Integration of anima/animus facilitated through partnership
- Mature love requires ongoing individuation in both partners

### 2. Psychological Transformation Through Intimacy
How the demands, conflicts, and depths of intimate relationship catalyze profound psychological change.

**Relevance to Pair Bonding**:
- Relationship triggers confrontation with shadow aspects
- Partner mirrors unintegrated parts of self
- Conflict as opportunity for transformation, not just problem
- Sustained intimacy requires continuous self-awareness and growth

### 3. Transcendence of Ego in Union
Movement beyond ego-centered consciousness toward transpersonal awareness through intimate bonding.

**Relevance to Pair Bonding**:
- Falling in love involves temporary transcendence of ego boundaries
- Sexual union can facilitate transpersonal consciousness
- Long-term love requires balancing ego and transcendent dimensions
- Codependence as failed transcendence; healthy union as authentic transcendence

### 4. Mutual Development and Co-Individuation
How partners can support (or hinder) each other's psychological and spiritual development.

**Relevance to Pair Bonding**:
- Healthy relationships enhance both partners' growth
- Codependent relationships arrest individuation
- Secure attachment supports autonomous development
- Balance between union and separateness essential for growth
