# Larsen (2023) - An Evolutionary Case for Polygyny to Counter Demographic Collapse

## Full Citation

Larsen, M. (2023). An evolutionary case for polygyny to counter demographic collapse. *Frontiers in Psychology, 14*, 1062950. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1062950

---

## Executive Summary

This provocative paper argues that declining fertility and rising singlehood in advanced, gender-equal societies (particularly Nordic countries) stem from dysfunctional mating practices constrained by enforced monogamy. Drawing on evolutionary psychology and sex ratio theory, Larsen proposes that permitting polygyny could counter demographic collapse by aligning modern mating practices with evolved psychological mechanisms. The paper synthesizes evolutionary history, demographic statistics, literary analysis, and sex ratio research to demonstrate that contemporary prosperity and gender equality activate what the author terms a "polygynous mindset" in women, leading to temporal polygyny (serial monogamy with high-value males), increased singlehood, and fertility collapse.

**Core Thesis:** Modern monogamy norms create mismatch between evolved mating psychology and contemporary social structures, resulting in demographic crisis. Polygyny, practiced throughout most of human evolutionary history, may offer a solution by accommodating women's evolved preferences for high-status mates while increasing overall fertility.

## Key Empirical Findings

### 1. Demographic Crisis in Nordic Countries

**Fertility Collapse:**
- Norwegian fertility rate: 1.96 → 1.48 (within one decade)
- Finnish fertility rate: 1.9 → 1.4
- EU average: 1.5 (well below replacement rate of 2.1)
- Projections: Spain, Japan, and 20+ nations on track to halve populations by 2100

**Singlehood Epidemic:**
- Scandinavia: World's highest one-person households (43-46%)
- From 1985-2012, Norwegian men failing to reproduce by age 45: 14% → 23%
- Involuntary childlessness affects men **3x more** than women
- Denmark: 45% of low-skilled men live alone

**Page 1-2:** "Scandinavia has the world's highest occurrence of one-person households: 43–46%... In the past decade, the Norwegian fertility rate dropped from 2.0 to 1.5."

---

### 2. Mate Value Stratification

**Economic Status and Pair-Bonding (Norway):**
- High-salary men: 90% pair-bonded by age 40
- Low-salary men: 40% pair-bonded by age 40
- Stark stratification: Most high-value males monopolize mating opportunities

**U.S. Parallel Trends:**
- Young men's sexual inactivity: 19% → 31% (over two decades)
- Male virginity under age 30: 8% → 27% (2008-2018)
- Rising incel phenomenon: Sexual exclusion of low-status males

**Page 7:** "Norwegian men with high salaries have a 90% chance of being pair-bonded by age 40—those with low salaries, a 40% chance."

---

### 3. Temporal Polygyny ("Recycling" High-Value Males)

**Mechanism:**
- Women serially pair with high-status men rather than settle for lower-status partners
- Experts attribute unequal childlessness to "women's recycling of high-value mates"
- Temporal polygyny: Sequential rather than simultaneous polygyny

**Behavioral Pattern:**
- Women raise mate standards in response to perceived partner scarcity
- Increased promiscuity + increased selectivity (paradoxical but predicted by sex ratio theory)
- Temporal sharing of high-value males preferred over permanent pairing with low-value males

**Page 7:** "Experts attribute this inequality to women's recycling of high-value mates (Jensen and Østby, 2014)—which can be viewed as a form of temporal polygyny."

## Theoretical Framework

### Sex Ratio Theory

**Core Concept:**
- Perceived sex ratio (PSR) determines mating strategies
- PSR = subjective sense of partner availability, not just numerical ratio
- Women in prosperous societies perceive partner scarcity despite equal sex ratios (few "acceptable" partners)

**Predictions:**
- **Female-biased PSR** (perceived shortage of quality males):
  - Women become more promiscuous (compete for access to high-value males)
  - Women raise mate standards (hypergamy intensifies)
  - Polygynous mindset activated
  - Men reduce commitment (abundant options)
- **Male-biased PSR** (perceived shortage of women):
  - Men increase courtship effort
  - Women demand higher commitment
  - Monogamy/long-term pairing increases

**Page 3:** "Sex ratio theory predicts how male and female mating psychologies will respond to a perceived surplus or scarcity of potential partners."

---

### Evoked Culture vs. Transmitted Culture (Cosmides & Tooby, 1992)

**Two Sources of Cultural Variation:**

**1. Transmitted Culture:**
- Learned through socialization, teaching, imitation
- Norms, beliefs, customs passed between generations
- Example: Monogamy norms, marriage laws, sexual morality

**2. Evoked Culture:**
- Universal psychological mechanisms responding to environmental cues
- Context-dependent activation of evolved strategies
- Example: Polygynous mindset triggered by perceived partner scarcity + prosperity

**Larsen's Application:**
- Modern Nordic societies transmit monogamy norms (transmitted culture)
- But prosperity + gender equality evoke polygynous psychology (evoked culture)
- **Mismatch:** Cultural norms conflict with evoked psychology → dysfunction

**Page 3:** "Our diverse hominin past has facilitated a range of mating strategies; local circumstances affect which are activated. The behaviors that these activated strategies motivate aggregate to what Cosmides and Tooby (1992) termed evoked culture."

---

### Strategic Pluralism Theory Connection

**Larsen's Framework Aligns with SPT:**
- Humans possess flexible mating strategies (not fixed)
- Strategies activated by ecological/social context
- Trade-offs between mating effort and parenting effort
- Within-sex variation adaptive (different strategies succeed in different contexts)

**Modern Context Activates:**
- Female choosiness (hypergamy)
- Temporal polygyny (serial high-value male pairing)
- Male status competition (winner-take-all dynamics)
- Low-status male exclusion

**Integration:**
> "Strategic Pluralism Theory (Gangestad & Simpson, 2000) predicts that humans evolved conditional mating strategies. Larsen (2023) extends this by showing how modern socioeconomic conditions—prosperity, gender equality, monogamy constraints—evoke specific strategic responses: women's polygynous mindset, men's status competition, and demographic collapse as unintended consequence."

## Evolutionary History: Hominin Mating Patterns

### From Chimpanzee LCA to Modern Humans

**1. Promiscuity (6+ million years ago):**
- Last common ancestor with chimpanzees
- Multi-male, multi-female mating
- Sperm competition primary

**2. Polygyny (Agricultural Period):**
- Agricultural revolution enabled wealth accumulation
- Status differentiation increased
- High-status males monopolized multiple wives
- Practiced across most agricultural societies

**3. Monogamy (Christian/Modern West):**
- Christian ideology sacralized monogamous marriage
- Legal enforcement of monogamy (bigamy laws)
- Industrial modernity: Gender equality + welfare state
- Recent: "Confluent love" ideology (self-realization, reward-maximization)

**Larsen's Argument:**
- Humans evolved under diverse mating systems (6+ million years)
- Psychological mechanisms adapted to **strategic flexibility**
- Monogamy hegemony is recent and culturally imposed
- Current dysfunction: Enforced monogamy + evolved polygynous psychology = mismatch

**Page 1:** "More than 6 million years of hominin evolution under promiscuous, polygynous, and monogamous regimes shaped mate preferences that evoke different cultural and behavioral responses as environments change."

## Literary Analysis: Nordic Mating Ideologies

### Biocultural Methodology

**Approach:**
- Analyzes Nordic literary works (past millennium)
- Traces evolution of mating ideologies
- Shows how narratives shape reproductive norms

**Three Ideological Phases:**

**1. Christian Marriage (Medieval-19th Century):**
- Marriage as sacred, permanent bond
- Reproduction as duty
- Divorce prohibited or highly stigmatized

**2. Romantic Love (19th-20th Century):**
- Idealization of passionate love
- Emphasis on emotional connection
- Tension between duty and desire

**3. Confluent Love (Late 20th Century-Present):**
- **Self-realization** as highest value
- Relationships must be **rewarding** (convenience)
- No obligation to incur costs (reproduction)
- Serial pair-bonding normalized

**Larsen's Critique:**
> "Confluent love ideology justifies not incurring the cost of reproduction."

**Page 2:** "In this article, I argue that the tenets of confluent love contribute to today's dysfunctional mating regime. Sacralizing self-realization, convenience, and reward, this mating ideology justifies not incurring the cost of reproduction."

## Female Mate Preferences and Hypergamy

### Evolved Preferences (Buss, 2016)

**Women Prefer:**
- High education and status
- Financial success
- Greater intelligence
- Tall stature
- Independence and self-confidence

**In Prosperous, Gender-Equal Societies:**
- Women less dependent on male provisioning
- **But:** Evolved preferences persist (not eliminated by economic independence)
- **Result:** Women can afford to wait for ideal partner (or remain single)

**Hypergamy Intensification:**
- Economic empowerment → women prioritize genetic quality over resources
- Predicted by Gangestad & Simpson (2000, p. 608)
- Validated by Bertrand et al. (2021) and Larsen (2023)

**Page 10:** "Being a high-value man in the modern environment is about more than financial capital... Women generally want men with high education and status, financial success, greater intelligence, a tall stature, independence, and self-confidence (Buss, 2016; DeSantis, 2021)."

## Male Stratification and Incel Phenomenon

### Winner-Take-All Dynamics

**High-Status Males:**
- Multiple sequential partnerships (temporal polygyny)
- Reproductive success concentrated
- Able to delay commitment (abundant options)

**Low-Status Males:**
- Increasingly excluded from mating market
- Rising virginity, sexual inactivity
- Involuntary childlessness

**Social Consequences:**
- **"Young male syndrome"**: Frustration → risk-taking, violence, crime
- Political radicalization
- Mental health crisis

**Henrich (2020) Concerns:**
- Polygyny increases male-male competition
- Zero-sum mindset (more competitors for scarce mates)
- Social instability from sexually excluded young men

**Larsen's Response:**
- These costs **already exist** under temporal polygyny
- Formalizing polygyny might **reduce** dysfunction by clarifying expectations
- Current system: Worst of both worlds (high competition + no stability)

**Page 10:** "With today's enforced monogamy, more women are relegated to mate with low-status men, share higher-status men in temporal succession, or be single."

## Policy Implications and Recommendations

### Case for Legalizing Polygyny

**Rationale:**
1. **Demographic:** Could increase fertility rates
2. **Social:** Reduce singlehood epidemic
3. **Psychological:** Align culture with evolved psychology
4. **Pragmatic:** Other interventions (immigration, pronatalism) have failed

**Predicted Benefits (Table 1, p. 12):**
- Reduced singlehood
- Higher perceived sex ratio (scarcer single women)
- Increased fertility
- Fewer divorces (clearer expectations)
- Multi-parent households (more parental investment per child)
- Less "helicopter parenting" (parental effort distributed)

**Predicted Costs (Table 1, p. 12):**
- Increased male-male competition
- Fewer mating opportunities for low-status men
- Potential for social instability (young male frustration)
- Less economic growth (men pursuing wives, not careers)
- Risk of patriarchal oppression
- Commoditization of women

**Page 11:** "If (1) we conclude that quick depopulation is likely to have grave consequences for human well-being... and (2) we are unable to find other effective means for increasing fertility... we should give polygyny serious consideration."

---

### Current Policy Failures

**Immigration:**
- Recommended by most policymakers
- Fertility rates continued falling despite immigration
- Immigrant fertility rates converge to host country rates within 1-2 generations
- Some immigrant groups "undermine future viability of Western welfare states"
- Not sustainable long-term solution

**Pronatalism:**
- Financial incentives (child subsidies, parental leave)
- Minimal impact on fertility
- Addresses symptoms, not root cause (mating dysfunction)

**Economic Policies:**
- Housing assistance, childcare subsidies
- Help already-paired couples have more children
- Don't address core problem: **Not pairing in the first place**

**Larsen's Assessment:**
- Conventional policies inadequate
- Avoiding polygyny discussion due to moral taboos
- Need "more imaginative debate" beyond status quo

**Page 12:** "Other recommendations are oddly absent, as is constructive debate (Eberstadt, 2021)... we owe ourselves and future generations a more imaginative debate around our era's demographic collapse than what we have had so far."

## Critical Evaluation

### Strengths

**1. Interdisciplinary Integration:**
- Evolutionary psychology + demography + literary analysis
- Bridges biological, psychological, sociological, and cultural dimensions
- Rare combination of quantitative data and qualitative cultural analysis

**2. Policy Courage:**
- Addresses taboo subject (polygyny) with empirical rigor
- Refuses to let moral panic override demographic reality
- Calls for "more imaginative debate" rather than status quo acceptance

**3. Empirical Grounding:**
- Rich Nordic demographic data
- Cross-national comparisons
- Literary sources as cultural evidence
- Sex ratio theory well-supported

**4. Theoretical Coherence:**
- Evoked vs. transmitted culture distinction clarifies mechanism
- Sex ratio theory provides clear predictions
- Strategic pluralism framework integrates findings

---

### Limitations

**1. Causality Ambiguity:**
- Correlational evidence (can't prove polygyny would increase fertility)
- Confounding variables (e.g., urbanization, secularization, economic insecurity)
- No experimental or quasi-experimental data

**2. Unintended Consequences:**
- Acknowledged but not fully explored
- Young male syndrome risk (violence, radicalization)
- Potential for patriarchal regression
- Unknown feedback loops in complex system

**3. Alternative Explanations Underexplored:**
- Economic factors: Housing costs, job insecurity, student debt
- Technological: Contraception, social media, dating apps
- Cultural: Individualism, consumerism, hedonism
- Reduced to mating psychology (may be overly reductive)

**4. Policy Feasibility:**
- Legal polygyny politically impossible in most Western democracies
- Religious opposition (Christian, secular humanist)
- Feminist opposition (patriarchy concerns)
- Public opinion strongly negative

**5. WEIRD Sample Bias:**
- Focus on Nordic countries (extreme case)
- May not generalize to other Western societies
- Nordic cultural factors (egalitarianism, secularism) may be unique

## Conclusion

Larsen (2023) provides the most explicit and policy-oriented analysis of the demographic crisis resulting from mismatch between evolved mating psychology and modern social structures. By proposing polygyny as a solution, Larsen forces scholarly and public discourse to confront the implications of evolutionary psychology for policy—a conversation most sources avoid. While politically unfeasible and potentially problematic, the polygyny proposal serves as a valuable thought experiment revealing the depth of the mismatch between biology and culture in contemporary mating.

For the dissertation, Larsen's work:
1. **Quantifies the crisis** (demographic data from Nordic countries)
2. **Explicates the mechanism** (sex ratio theory, evoked culture, polygynous mindset)
3. **Connects to all sources** (Buss, Gangestad & Simpson, Bertrand, Jung, Miller)
4. **Raises policy stakes** (not just individual unhappiness but civilizational collapse)
5. **Sets up transcendent solution** (structural fixes inadequate; need conscious transformation)

**Key Takeaway:** If Gangestad & Simpson (2000) explain **why** mismatch occurs, and Bertrand et al. (2021) document **economic** consequences, Larsen (2023) reveals **demographic** consequences and forces the question: Can structural solutions (polygyny) fix evolved mismatch, or does resolution require conscious transcendence (Sacred Bonds)?
