# Buss (1989) - Sex Differences in Human Mate Preferences

**Citation:** Buss, D. M. (1989). Sex differences in human mate preferences: Evolutionary hypotheses tested in 37 cultures. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 12(1), 1-49.

**Processing Date:** 2025-12-11
**Status:** Complete - Landmark Empirical Study

---

## Executive Summary

David Buss's 1989 study is THE landmark empirical work documenting universal sex differences in mate preferences across 37 cultures and 10,047 participants.

### Key Findings

**Women Universally Value:**
1. **Good financial prospects** (significant in 36/37 cultures)
2. **Ambition/industriousness** (significant in 29/37 cultures)
3. **Age** (prefer older partners - all 37 cultures)
4. **Dependability, emotional stability, intelligence**

**Men Universally Value:**
1. **Physical attractiveness** (significant in all 37 cultures)
2. **Youth** (prefer younger partners - all 37 cultures)
3. **Chastity** (variable by culture)

### Theoretical Significance

**Confirms evolutionary predictions:**
- Women's preferences match need for resources (parental investment)
- Men's preferences match fertility cues (reproductive value)
- **Cross-cultural universality** = evolved mechanisms, not cultural learning

## Sample Characteristics

**Scale:** 37 cultures across 6 continents, 33 countries
**N = 10,047 participants**
- 4,615 men, 5,432 women
- Age range: 16-28 years (mean ~23)

**Cultures Included:**
- **Africa:** Nigeria, South Africa-Zulu, Zambia
- **Asia:** China, India, Indonesia, Iran, Israel (Palestinian), Japan, Taiwan
- **Europe:** Belgium, Bulgaria, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany (East & West), Great Britain, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Spain, Sweden, Yugoslavia
- **North America:** Canada (English & French), USA (mainland & Hawaii)
- **South America:** Brazil, Colombia, Venezuela
- **Oceania:** Australia, New Zealand

## Key Findings by Attribute

### Women's Preferences (Resource-Related)

**1. Good Financial Prospects**
- **Effect size:** Cohen's d = 0.90 (large)
- **Significant in:** 36 of 37 cultures
- **Interpretation:** Women value resource acquisition ability

**2. Ambition and Industriousness**  
- **Effect size:** d = 0.67 (medium-large)
- **Significant in:** 29 of 37 cultures
- **Interpretation:** Women value future earning potential

**3. Age Preference**
- **Finding:** Women prefer men 3.42 years OLDER (average)
- **Range:** 2.0 years (Finland) to 6.5 years (Zambia)
- **All 37 cultures:** Women prefer older men
- **Interpretation:** Older = more resources accumulated

**4. Social Status**
- **Significant sex difference** in most cultures
- Women value status more than men

### Men's Preferences (Fertility-Related)

**1. Physical Attractiveness**
- **Effect size:** d = -0.81 (large, men value MORE)
- **Significant in:** ALL 37 cultures
- **Interpretation:** Attractiveness signals fertility/health

**2. Age Preference**
- **Finding:** Men prefer women 2.66 years YOUNGER (average)
- **All 37 cultures:** Men prefer younger women
- **Interpretation:** Youth = higher reproductive value

**3. Chastity/Virginity**
- **High cultural variation**
- **Important in:** China, India, Indonesia, Iran, Taiwan
- **Unimportant in:** Sweden, Netherlands, Norway, Finland
- **Pattern:** More traditional cultures value chastity more

## Evolutionary Explanations

### Trivers (1972) Predictions Confirmed

**Women's High Investment:**
- 9+ months gestation + years of childcare
- **Prediction:** Should value resources, status, age (proxies for investment ability)
- **Result:** ✅ Confirmed across 37 cultures

**Men's Low Obligate Investment:**
- Minimum investment = gametes
- **Prediction:** Should value fertility cues (youth, beauty)
- **Result:** ✅ Confirmed across 37 cultures

### Why Cross-Cultural Universality Matters

**Buss's Argument:**
1. If preferences were purely cultural, we'd see random variation
2. Instead, we see PATTERNED consistency across diverse cultures
3. **Conclusion:** Preferences have evolved biological basis

**Alternative Explanations Ruled Out:**
- ❌ Social learning alone (why same pattern globally?)
- ❌ Economic structure alone (pattern holds in diverse economies)
- ✅ Evolved psychology shaped by ancestral selection pressures

## Limitations Acknowledged

1. **Sample:** Primarily university students (limits generalizability)
2. **Stated preferences:** May differ from revealed preferences in actual mating
3. **Snapshot in time:** 1980s data (but replicated in later studies)
4. **Some cultural variation:** Not all effects equal magnitude across cultures
