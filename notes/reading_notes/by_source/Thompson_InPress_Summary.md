## Research Overview

This qualitative study provides a critical multimodal discourse analysis of how OkCupid's design and interface features shape the representation and legitimization of consensual non-monogamous (CNM) relationships. While OkCupid has introduced inclusive features (expanded gender/sexuality/relationship options, profile linking), Thompson argues that platform design still reinforces **mononormativity** and privileges hierarchical, couple-centered forms of non-monogamy over more diverse relationship structures.

---

## Research Questions

1. **What types of sexuality normativities are constructed and (re)produced in digital dating contexts?**
2. **What are the consequences of the platform interface for self-representation and sexuality?**

---

## Theoretical Framework

### Primary Frameworks
- **Multimodal Critical Discourse Studies (MCDS):** Analyzes how various semiotic resources (text, images, interface design) produce ideological effects
- **Queer Linguistics:** Examines how language and discourse construct and contest sexual normativity
- **Mononormativity Theory:** Investigates how monogamous coupledom is positioned as natural, optimal, and morally superior

### Key Concepts
- **Mononormativity:** The dominant belief system establishing monogamous (often heterosexual) couples as the natural and morally loftier relationship form
- **Consensual Non-Monogamy (CNM):** Umbrella term for ethical non-monogamous practices including polyamory, swinging, and open relationships
- **Platform Affordances:** The possibilities and constraints that digital architecture creates for user behavior and self-presentation
- **Relationship Escalator:** The culturally scripted progression of intimate relationships toward exclusive pair-bonding

## Methodology

### Research Design
Qualitative multimodal discourse analysis combining:
- **In-depth interviews:** 57 CNM-identified participants (from larger sample of 100+)
- **Profile analysis:** 45 OkCupid profiles (23 linked profiles)
- **Participant observation:** Users editing profiles, swiping, messaging

### Recruitment
- Snowball sampling via community events, university flyers, social networks, social media
- Researcher positionality: Insider in non-monogamous and LGBTQ+ communities

### Demographics
- Participants primarily white, middle-class, educated
- 28 interview participants had OkCupid profiles; 6 used linking feature

---

## Major Findings

### 1. Visibility vs. Legitimization Paradox
- **Increased Visibility:** OkCupid's expanded menus (13 sexual orientations, 22 gender options, relationship orientation options) make CNM more visible
- **Persistent Marginalization:** Despite surface inclusivity, design features reinforce mononormative assumptions

### 2. Platform Design Reinforces Mononormativity

#### The Linking Feature Paradox
- **Validation Function:** Proves ethical non-monogamy (vs. cheating)
- **Limitation:** Only allows linking with *one* partner
- **Effect:** Privileges hierarchical CNM (primary/secondary structure) over networked or solo polyamory

#### Language and Interface
- **Singular Terminology:** Default language uses singular "partner," "my wife," "my husband"
- **Pair-Bonded Photo Norms:** Visual presentation defaults to couples
- **Menu Constraints:** Pre-populated options shape identity construction

### 3. Self-Representation Challenges
- Users must navigate between platform constraints and authentic self-expression
- Many CNM users default to mononormative language/presentation due to interface limitations
- Non-hierarchical polyamorous individuals find current features limiting and potentially erasing

### 4. Cultural and Commercial Motivations
- Platform changes reflect both:
  - Cultural shifts toward CNM acceptance
  - Commercial interests in attracting "lifelong daters" (people less likely to leave platforms after finding partners)

## Key Arguments and Conclusions

### Main Thesis
"While the addition of non-monogamy features on OkCupid appear to challenge mononormativity on the surface, the platform design and user discursive practices simultaneously reinscribe hegemonic coupledom as the preferred way to do relationships."

### Supporting Arguments
1. **Visibility ≠ Full Legitimization:** Including CNM options increases awareness but doesn't guarantee equitable representation
2. **Design is Ideological:** Interface features (linking limits, singular language) encode cultural assumptions about "normal" relationships
3. **Privileging Hierarchical CNM:** Platforms favor CNM structures closest to monogamy (couples with primaries) over more radical alternatives
4. **Power of Defaults:** What's not included in menus becomes invisible; defaults shape user behavior

### Positive Implications
- Integration of CNM labels on mainstream platforms is important progress
- Visibility can reduce stigma and linguistically authorize CNM as legitimate
- Platform evolution reflects broader cultural shifts

### Critical Limitations
- True inclusivity remains incomplete
- Design features subtly enforce hierarchies and couple privilege
- Non-hierarchical relationship models remain marginalized

## Relevance to Evolutionary Psychology and Mating Markets

### Cross-Cultural Context
- **Only 17% of human cultures are strictly monogamous** (Chapais 2013)
- **21% of U.S. adults** report having engaged in CNM relationships (Haupert et al. 2017)
- **At least 1.44 million U.S. adults** identify as polyamorous (Rubel & Burleigh 2020)

### Mating Strategy Diversity
- CNM practices reflect diversity in human mating strategies (sexual variety, coalition building, pair-bonding)
- Platform design influences which strategies are viable in contemporary digital mating markets
- Technological affordances shape evolutionary pressures on mate selection

### Digital Mating Market Dynamics
- **Growth:** 33.9 million U.S. users in 2018; projected 37.2 million by 2022
- **Marginalized Groups:** LGBTQ+ adults (55%) use dating platforms at nearly double the rate of heterosexual adults (28%)
- **Thin Markets:** People in niche mating markets especially rely on online platforms (Rosenfeld & Thomas 2012)

## Key Takeaways for Dissertation

1. **Technology is Not Neutral:** Platform design actively shapes which mating strategies and relationship structures are viable
2. **Visibility ≠ Equality:** Including marginalized identities in menus is progress but doesn't guarantee equitable representation
3. **Defaults Matter:** What platforms assume as "normal" through defaults and interface design powerfully shapes user behavior
4. **Cultural Lag:** Even as platforms evolve, they tend to encode traditional norms through subtle design choices
5. **Commercial Incentives:** Platform changes reflect business interests (retaining users) as much as social justice goals
6. **Authenticity Crisis:** Gap between authentic desires and allowable platform representation contributes to contemporary mating difficulties

## Limitations and Future Directions

### Study Limitations
- Sample primarily white, middle-class, educated
- Focus on OkCupid; findings may not generalize to all platforms
- Pre-publication; peer review process may refine arguments

### Future Research Needs
- Comparative analysis across multiple platforms
- More diverse demographic samples
- Longitudinal studies of platform evolution
- Quantitative measures of CNM visibility and outcomes
- Analysis of emerging platforms (Feeld, #Open) designed specifically for CNM users
