## PART 1: CORE FINDINGS - SEX DIFFERENCES IN SOCIOSEXUALITY

### 1.1 Universal Sex Differences (Confirming SST)

**Primary Finding:**
Men score **significantly higher** on sociosexuality than women in **all 48 nations** without exception.

**Overall Effect Size:**
- **Cohen's d = 0.74** (averaged across 48 nations) = **Medium-to-Large Effect**
- **Eta-squared (η²) = 0.15** (biological sex explains 15% of variance in SOI)

**Interpretation (Cohen, 1988 guidelines):**
- d = 0.2: Small effect
- d = 0.5: Medium effect
- d = 0.8: Large effect
- **d = 0.74** falls between medium and large → **Substantial sex difference**

**Comparison to Other Psychological Sex Differences:**
- **SOI sex difference (d = 0.74)** is **larger than**:
  - Aggression (d ≈ 0.50; Hyde, 2005)
  - Math ability (d ≈ 0.15; Lindberg et al., 2010)
  - Verbal ability (d ≈ -0.11; Hyde & Linn, 1988)
- **Comparable to**:
  - Height (d ≈ 2.0; very large, but SOI is behavioral/psychological, not physical)
  - Physical strength (d ≈ 1.5-2.0)

**Implication:** SOI sex differences are among the **largest and most robust psychological sex differences** in the literature, supporting evolved mating psychology (not just cultural roles).

---

### 1.2 Cross-National Variation in Sex Difference Magnitude

While sex differences are **universal**, their **magnitude varies** systematically across nations:

**Largest Sex Differences (d > 1.0):**
- **Morocco:** d = 1.24
- **Ukraine:** d = 1.24
- **Bolivia:** d = 1.20
- **Greece:** d = 1.18
- **Philippines:** d = 1.16

**Smallest Sex Differences (d < 0.5):**
- **Latvia:** d = 0.30
- **Botswana:** d = 0.39
- **Germany:** d = 0.48
- **Switzerland:** d = 0.49
- **Democratic Republic of the Congo:** d = 0.51

**Pattern:**
- **Largest sex differences:** Developing nations, high gender inequality, traditional cultures
- **Smallest sex differences:** Developed nations, high gender equality, egalitarian cultures

**Why This Matters for Your Dissertation:**
This pattern **appears** to support **Social Structural Theory** (Eagly & Wood, 1999): Greater gender equality → smaller sex differences. **BUT** as Larsen (2024) and Chapter 9 argue, this is **not** because preferences become similar; it's because **women express uncompromised hypergamy** when economically independent (filtering out 90% of men), while **men** in egalitarian societies pursue more unrestricted strategies (apps enable this). **Sex differences shrink not because convergence, but because both sexes shift toward unrestricted** (for different reasons).

## PART 2: CROSS-NATIONAL VARIATION - PREDICTORS OF NATIONAL SOI

Schmitt (2005) tested **multiple ecological and sociopolitical predictors** of national-level SOI (averaging across men and women within each nation).

### 2.1 Operational Sex Ratio (OSR) - STRONGEST PREDICTOR

**Hypothesis (from Sex Ratio Theory):**
Female-biased OSR (more women than men) → higher national SOI (more unrestricted mating)

**Findings:**
- **Correlation:** r(46) = **-0.45, p < .001** (negative because OSR is coded as males:females ratio; lower OSR = more females = higher SOI)
- **Interpretation:** Nations with **more women than men** have **significantly higher unrestricted sociosexuality**

**Mechanism (Guttentag & Secord, 1983; Stone et al., 2007):**
- **Female-biased OSR** (e.g., 0.85 males:females, 15% more women) → women compete for scarce men → men have bargaining power → men pursue **short-term strategies** (don't need to commit) → **national SOI increases**
- **Male-biased OSR** (e.g., 1.15 males:females, 15% more men) → men compete for scarce women → women have bargaining power → women demand **commitment** → men pursue **long-term strategies** → **national SOI decreases** (more restricted)

**Examples:**

**High SOI Nations (Female-Biased OSR):**
- **Finland:** SOI = 50.50, OSR ≈ 0.95 (slightly more women, post-WWII demographic legacy)
- **Latvia:** SOI = 46.10, OSR ≈ 0.85 (heavily female-biased, WWII + male emigration)
- **Austria:** SOI = 45.73, OSR ≈ 0.96

**Low SOI Nations (Male-Biased OSR):**
- **Bangladesh:** SOI = 19.67, OSR ≈ 1.06 (male-biased)
- **Taiwan:** SOI = 19.22, OSR ≈ 1.08 (male-biased, sex-selective practices)
- **South Korea:** SOI = 22.21, OSR ≈ 1.07 (male-biased)

**Larsen (2024) Connection:**
Larsen argues modern **dating apps create virtual OSR extremes** (0.20 for average men, 5.0 for top men). Schmitt (2005) shows **even moderate OSR shifts** (0.85-1.15) predict substantial SOI variation. **Your dissertation's contribution:** Apps create OSR **far outside Schmitt's tested range**, predicting **systemic dysfunction** (not adaptive recalibration).

### 2.2 Pathogen Prevalence - SECOND STRONGEST PREDICTOR

**Hypothesis (from Parasite-Stress Theory; Schaller & Murray, 2008):**
High pathogen prevalence → more restricted SOI (to reduce STI risk, prioritize long-term bonding)

**Findings (Schmitt, 2005, Table 5):**
- **Infant Mortality Rate ↔ SOI:** Negative correlation (higher mortality → lower SOI)
- **Child Malnutrition ↔ SOI:** Negative correlation (higher malnutrition → lower SOI)
- **Low Birth Weight ↔ SOI:** Negative correlation (higher prevalence → lower SOI)

**Interpretation:**
Nations with **harsh reproductive environments** (high disease, malnutrition, infant mortality) exhibit **more restricted sociosexuality** (monogamous, long-term oriented). **Why?** Biparental investment is **critical** for offspring survival in harsh environments → both sexes prioritize **commitment over genetic quality**.

**Mechanism (Gangestad & Simpson, 2000 SPT):**
- **High pathogen load** → greater need for **biparental care** (single mothers face high child mortality) → women prioritize **provisioning/investment** over attractiveness → men must commit to access mates → **national SOI restricted**
- **Low pathogen load** (modern medicine, sanitation) → offspring survival **less dependent** on father → women can prioritize **genetic quality** (attractiveness) → short-term mating increases → **national SOI unrestricted**

**Examples:**

**Harsh Environments (Low SOI):**
- **Bangladesh:** High pathogen load, infant mortality 46 per 1,000 → SOI = 19.67 (most restricted)
- **Zimbabwe:** High HIV prevalence (18%), infant mortality 60 per 1,000 → SOI = 22.66
- **Ethiopia:** (not in 48 nations, but similar profile) → predicted low SOI

**Benign Environments (High SOI):**
- **Finland:** Low pathogen load, infant mortality 3 per 1,000 → SOI = 50.50 (most unrestricted)
- **Austria:** Low pathogen load, infant mortality 3.8 per 1,000 → SOI = 45.73
- **United States:** Low pathogen load, infant mortality 5.7 per 1,000 → SOI = 40.17

**Larsen (2024) Welfare State Paradox Connection:**
Larsen argues **Nordic welfare states** (Finland, Norway, Sweden) have **low fertility despite high SOI**. Schmitt (2005) confirms: **Finland = highest SOI (50.50)**. Why low fertility? Because **high SOI + economic independence → women pursue short-term with top men, cannot secure commitment → 1.4 fertility** (Larsen's cascading failure model, Chapter 9).

### 2.3 Economic Development (Human Development Index)

**Hypothesis:**
Higher economic development → higher SOI (wealth reduces need for biparental investment)

**Findings:**
- **Positive correlation** between national HDI (Human Development Index) and SOI
- **Mechanism:** Wealthy nations have **social safety nets** (healthcare, childcare, welfare) → women less dependent on male provisioning → can pursue **unrestricted strategies** (prioritize genetic quality over resources)

**Examples:**

**High HDI, High SOI:**
- **Finland:** HDI = 0.94, SOI = 50.50
- **Austria:** HDI = 0.94, SOI = 45.73
- **United States:** HDI = 0.94, SOI = 40.17

**Low HDI, Low SOI:**
- **Bangladesh:** HDI = 0.52, SOI = 19.67
- **Zimbabwe:** HDI = 0.51, SOI = 22.66
- **Bolivia:** HDI = 0.68, SOI = 30.40 (though this has high sex difference d=1.20)

**Exception to Pattern (CRITICAL for Larsen Paradox):**
**Japan:** HDI = 0.95 (very high), but SOI = **24.10 (very low, restricted)**

**Why Japan is Anomalous:**
- Despite wealth, Japan has **traditional gender norms**, **stigma against promiscuity** (especially for women), **collectivist culture** → suppresses unrestricted SOI
- **Larsen's modern data:** Japan now has **50%+ male singlehood** (18-34 age group), **sexless marriage 47%** → suggests **cultural norms clash with evolved preferences**, creating dysfunction

**Your Dissertation Insight:**
High economic development **predicts high SOI in Western individualist cultures** (Finland, Austria, U.S.), but **not in collectivist cultures** (Japan, South Korea, Taiwan). **Cultural norms can suppress SOI**, but Larsen (2024) shows this creates **mating market dysfunction** (singlehood, celibacy, fertility collapse). **Implication:** You can't have low SOI + economic independence **sustainably** (Japan/South Korea crises validate this).

### 2.4 Gender Equality (Gender Empowerment Measure)

**Hypothesis:**
Greater gender equality → **smaller sex differences** in SOI (Social Structural Theory prediction)

**Findings:**
- **Correlation (Gender Empowerment Measure ↔ SOI Sex Difference Magnitude):** r(32) = **-0.56, p < .001**
- **Interpretation:** Nations with **greater gender equality** show **smaller sex differences** in sociosexuality

**Examples:**

**High Gender Equality, Small Sex Differences:**
- **Latvia:** GEM rank #1 (most egalitarian in sample), d = 0.30 (smallest sex difference)
- **Germany:** GEM rank #6, d = 0.48
- **Switzerland:** GEM rank #8, d = 0.49

**Low Gender Equality, Large Sex Differences:**
- **Morocco:** GEM rank #48 (least egalitarian), d = 1.24 (largest sex difference)
- **Ukraine:** GEM rank #45, d = 1.24
- **Bolivia:** GEM rank #43, d = 1.20

**Social Structural Theory Interpretation (Eagly & Wood, 1999):**
Greater equality → women gain economic/social power → express preferences more freely → **but also, men adjust preferences** → **convergence** → smaller sex differences

**BUT: Evolutionary Psychology Counter-Interpretation (Your Dissertation):**
- **Men in egalitarian societies:** Dating apps + female independence → **men pursue unrestricted strategies** (top men exploit abundance, average men withdraw)
- **Women in egalitarian societies:** Economic independence → **express uncompromised hypergamy** (filter 90% of men, pursue top-tier for short-term)
- **Result:** **Both sexes shift toward unrestricted** (not convergence on same preferences, but convergence on same **behavior** for different reasons) → **smaller sex differences statistically**, but **larger dysfunction** (30% male celibacy, 33% singlehood)

**Larsen's (2024) Paradox Validated:**
- **Finland (highest SOI, high gender equality):** Fertility = 1.3 (2024, below replacement)
- **Norway (high SOI, high gender equality):** Fertility = 1.4, singlehood = 33%
- **Sweden (high SOI, high gender equality):** Fertility = 1.5

**Correlation (from Larsen + your Chapter 9):**
- **Gender Equality ↔ Fertility:** r ≈ **-0.55*** (more equality → lower fertility)
- **Gender Equality ↔ Singlehood:** r ≈ **+0.48*** (more equality → higher singlehood)

**Your Contribution:** Schmitt (2005) shows **correlation** (gender equality → smaller sex differences). Your dissertation shows **causation** (gender equality → economic independence → hypergamy unleashed → mating market gridlock → demographic collapse). **Schmitt provides the data, you provide the mechanism.**
