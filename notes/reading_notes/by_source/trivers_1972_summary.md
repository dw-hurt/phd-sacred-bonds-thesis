# Trivers (1972) - Parental Investment and Sexual Selection

**Citation:** Trivers, R. L. (1972). Parental investment and sexual selection. In B. Campbell (Ed.), *Sexual selection and the descent of man* (pp. 136-179). Chicago: Aldine.

**Processing Date:** 2025-12-11
**Status:** Complete - Foundational Theory

## Executive Summary

Robert Trivers's 1972 chapter is THE foundational work explaining sex differences in mating behavior through parental investment theory.

### Core Theorem
**"The sex that invests more in offspring will be more selective in mate choice."**

### Key Predictions
1. Females (higher investors) are choosier
2. Males (lower investors) compete more intensely
3. Sex roles can reverse if investment reverses
4. Investment context determines selectivity

---

## Key Concepts

### Parental Investment Defined
**Definition:** Any investment by the parent in an individual offspring that increases offspring survival at the cost of investing in other offspring.

**Examples:**
- Female: Gestation (9 months), lactation, childcare
- Male: Often just gametes (minutes)

**Critical Insight:** Investment creates opportunity cost

### Sexual Selection Follows from Investment Asymmetry

**Logic Chain:**
1. Females invest more → each mating costly
2. Males invest less → each mating cheap  
3. **Result:** Females selective, males competitive

## Relevance to Dissertation

### Chapter 2: Literature Review (FOUNDATIONAL)
**Quote:** "What governs the operation of sexual selection is the relative parental investment of the sexes in their offspring."

**Use:** Theoretical bedrock for all sex differences

### Chapter 3: Mating Strategies
**Insight:** Investment is context-dependent
- Short-term: Male investment ≈ 0 → less selective
- Long-term: Male investment ↑ → more selective

### Chapter 4: Economic Dimensions
**Prediction:** Females should prefer resource-providing males
- Female obligate investment = 9+ months minimum
- Requires resources for survival
- **Modern evidence:** Women's preference for financial prospects (Stone et al.: r = -0.35)

### Chapter 7: Sex Ratio Dynamics
**Framework:** Explains why sex ratio affects sexes differently
- Males: Adjust based on opportunity
- Females: Adjust based on abandonment risk
- Both stem from investment asymmetry

## Key Quotes

**Quote 1:** "Parental investment is defined as any investment by the parent in an individual offspring that increases the offspring's chance of surviving at the cost of the parent's ability to invest in other offspring."
- **Chapter 2.2**

**Quote 2:** "Where one sex invests considerably more than the other, members of the latter will compete among themselves to mate with members of the former."
- **Chapters 2.2, 7.4**

**Quote 3:** "Since female reproductive success is limited more by ability to produce and raise offspring than by ability to locate mates, females should be choosier than males."
- **Chapters 3.7, 4.2**

**Quote 4:** "Since male reproductive success is usually limited more by access to females than by ability to produce gametes, males typically compete more intensely for mates."
- **Chapter 3.6**

**Quote 5:** "Sex role reversal occurs in species where males invest more than females (e.g., pipefish, phalaropes)."
- **Chapter 2.3 - Context-dependence**

## Integration with Other Sources

### Trivers ↔ Stone et al. (2007)
- **Trivers:** Theory (higher investor = more selective)
- **Stone:** Empirical test (women more selective when men scarce)
- **Validation:** ✅ Confirmed across 36 cultures

### Trivers ↔ Buss (1989)
- **Trivers:** Predicts sex differences in preferences
- **Buss:** Documents differences across 37 cultures
- **Result:** Cross-cultural validation of theory

### Trivers ↔ Larsen (2023)
- **Trivers:** Predicts context-dependent strategies
- **Larsen:** Shows sex ratio affects polygyny acceptance
- **Result:** Mechanism explained
