## 🎯 STRATEGIC IMPORTANCE

Gangestad & Simpson (2000) COMPLETES Chapter 3 by explaining **why individuals within each sex vary in mating strategies**. While Buss & Schmitt (1993) established universal dual strategies, Strategic Pluralism Theory explains individual differences around those universals.

### **3. STONE ET AL. (2007) - Sex Ratio Effects**

**Relationship:** Sex ratio = real-time calibration cue (like developmental environment)

**Prediction:** Individual SOI should moderate sex ratio sensitivity:
- High-SOI men in male-surplus → extremely lowered standards
- Low-SOI men → maintain standards regardless
- High-SOI women in male-scarcity → extremely raised standards
- Low-SOI women → less affected

**Chapter 7 Integration:** Add Section 7.3 on individual differences in sex ratio sensitivity.

---

### **4. BERTRAND ET AL. (2015) - Economic Behavior**

**Relationship:** High-earning women's mating penalties vary by SOI

**Integration:** Restricted women face severe career-mating trade-offs (can't marry down, won't date casually). Unrestricted women face milder trade-offs (can date casually while pursuing career).

**Chapter 4 Integration:** Add SOI dimension to Section 4.4 on economic manifestations.

---

### **5. LARSEN (2023) - Modern Mating Crisis**

**Relationship:** Individual vulnerability varies by SOI

**Key Insight:** Mating crisis hits differently:
- **Restricted women:** Seek commitment men won't provide (HIGH vulnerability)
- **Restricted men:** Outcompeted by unrestricted men (HIGHEST vulnerability)  
- **Unrestricted men (high-condition):** Thrive in hookup culture (LOW vulnerability)
- **Unrestricted men (low-condition):** Desire casual sex but lack success (HIGH vulnerability)

**Chapter 9 Integration:** Add Section 9.4 on individual vulnerability to crisis.
