## Abstract

Operational sex ratio—the ratio of men to women in the mating pool—varies across cultures and can be expected to affect mate preference standards. Two hypotheses were examined: Classical (excess sex lowers standards) and Alternative (men lower standards when women abundant; women raise standards when men scarce). Using 15 mate characteristics from 9,809 people across 36 cultures, men showed general negative relationship (r = -0.18) supporting Classical Hypothesis. Women showed negative relationship for 12/15 characteristics (r = -0.14), with chastity positively related to sex ratio, supporting Alternative Hypothesis. Effects held after controlling for GNP.

## Research Methods Summary

**Design:** Cross-sectional, correlational

**Sample:**
- N = 9,809 participants
- 36 cultures (6 continents)
- Age: 14-70 years

**Measures:**
- 15 mate preference attributes (0-3 scale)
- Sex ratio: (Men 20-39) / (Women 15-34) × 100

**Analysis:**
- Correlation: Sex ratio × preferences
- Separate by sex
- Control for GNP per capita

---

## Key Findings

**Men:** r = -0.18 (p < 0.001) - All 15 attributes negative

**Women:** r = -0.14 (p < 0.05) - 12/15 attributes negative
- Financial prospects: r = -0.35 (strongest)
- Chastity: r = +0.29 (exception)

**Cross-cultural:** Effects held across 36 cultures, independent of economic development

## Limitations

1. Cross-sectional design (cannot establish causation)
2. Stated vs. revealed preferences  
3. Sex ratio timing assumptions
4. Mechanism unspecified
