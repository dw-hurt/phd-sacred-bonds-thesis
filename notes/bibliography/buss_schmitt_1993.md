## 🎯 ABSTRACT SUMMARY

**Original Abstract:**
> "This article proposes a contextual-evolutionary theory of human mating strategies. Both men and women are hypothesized to have evolved distinct psychological mechanisms that underlie short-term and long-term strategies. Nine hypotheses were derived regarding sex differences in the desire for sexual variety, in standards for short-term mates, in the prioritization of specific mate characteristics, and in willingness to consent to sex. Empirical tests supported most of the hypotheses. The theory accounts for both universal and context-dependent mate preferences, provides a framework for understanding sexual conflict, and integrates diverse findings on jealousy, mate attraction, and mate retention."

**Key Takeaway:**
Sexual Strategies Theory (SST) proposes that humans have evolved **dual mating strategies** (short-term and long-term) that are activated **context-dependently**. Men and women face different adaptive problems in each mating context, leading to predictable sex differences in:
- Desire for sexual variety (men: 18 lifetime partners; women: 4-5)
- Standards for mates (men lower short-term standards; women maintain high standards)
- Jealousy triggers (men: sexual infidelity; women: emotional infidelity)
- Sources of sexual conflict (strategic interference between sexes)

## 🔬 KEY FINDINGS & CONTRIBUTIONS

### **Major Theoretical Contributions**

1. **Strategic Pluralism Framework**
   - Both sexes possess multiple mating strategies (not just one)
   - Strategies shift based on temporal context (short-term vs. long-term)
   - First theory to formalize context-dependent mating psychology

2. **The 9 Core Hypotheses**
   - H1: Men desire more sexual variety than women
   - H2: Men lower standards for short-term mates
   - H3: Men evolved mechanisms to identify sexually accessible women
   - H4: Men prioritize fertility cues (youth, beauty)
   - H5: Women prioritize resource cues (financial prospects, ambition)
   - H6: Preferences shift across temporal contexts
   - H7: Men experience more sexual jealousy; women more emotional jealousy
   - H8: Sexual conflict arises from strategic interference
   - H9: Mate retention tactics reflect sex-specific threats

3. **Desire as Causal Foundation**
   - Sexual desire is the "engine" driving all mating behavior
   - Desires of one sex shape effective attraction tactics of opposite sex
   - Incompatible desires create sexual conflict

4. **Domain-Specific Adaptation Approach**
   - Not general "mating psychology"
   - Specific evolved mechanisms for specific adaptive problems
   - Example: Paternity uncertainty → specialized sexual jealousy mechanism

### **Major Empirical Findings**

| **Finding** | **Men** | **Women** | **Interpretation** |
|-------------|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Desired partners (2 years)** | 8 | 1 | Men's short-term orientation |
| **Desired partners (lifetime)** | 18 | 4-5 | 4× sex difference |
| **Stranger sex acceptance** | 75% | 0% | Men lower short-term standards |
| **Cross-cultural mate preferences** | Youth, beauty (37/37) | Resources, status (36/37) | Universal patterns |
| **Jealousy: Sexual infidelity worse** | 60% | 15% | Men's paternity uncertainty |
| **Jealousy: Emotional infidelity worse** | 40% | 85% | Women's resource loss |

### **Methodological Innovations**

- **Multi-method convergence:** Surveys, behavioral studies, physiological measures, cross-cultural data
- **Cross-cultural replication:** Jealousy findings replicated in Germany, Netherlands, Korea
- **Behavioral validation:** Clark & Hatfield (1989) stranger sex study
- **Longitudinal perspective:** Desired partners across multiple time intervals
- **Context manipulation:** Short-term vs. long-term preference shifts

## 🔗 RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER SOURCES

### **Theoretical Lineage**

**FOUNDATIONS (What SST Builds On):**
1. **Trivers (1972)** - Parental Investment and Sexual Selection
   - Relationship: SST **operationalizes** Trivers's abstract theory
   - Integration: "Trivers identified the *what* (parental investment asymmetry); SST specifies the *how* (dual strategies, context-dependent activation)"

2. **Symons (1979)** - The Evolution of Human Sexuality
   - Relationship: Symons provided conceptual groundwork; SST formalized it
   - Integration: Symons's insights → testable SST framework

3. **Buss (1989)** - Sex Differences in Human Mate Preferences (37 Cultures)
   - Relationship: SST **explains** Buss's empirical findings
   - Integration: "Buss (1989) documented *what* people prefer; SST explains *why* and *when* preferences change"

**EXTENSIONS (What Builds on SST):**
1. **Gangestad & Simpson (2000)** - Strategic Pluralism Theory
   - Relationship: **Extends SST** by adding individual differences (sociosexuality)
   - Integration: "SST established universal mechanisms; Strategic Pluralism explains individual variation"

2. **Stone et al. (2007)** - Sex Ratio and Mate Preferences
   - Relationship: **Empirically tests** SST's contextual predictions
   - Integration: "Stone et al. confirmed SST's temporal context effects manifest as ecological context effects (sex ratio)"

3. **Schmitt (2005)** - Sociosexuality Across 48 Nations
   - Relationship: **Cross-cultural test** of SST + individual differences
   - Integration: Sex differences in sociosexuality universal (48/48 nations), validating SST

4. **Buss (2023)** - When Men Behave Badly
   - Relationship: **Modern application** of SST to harmful behaviors
   - Integration: Strategic interference → sexual coercion, harassment, mate guarding violence

**MODERN APPLICATIONS:**
1. **Larsen (2023)** - Why Love Hurts
   - Relationship: Applies SST to **contemporary mating crisis**
   - Integration: "Dating apps activate short-term mating context → strategic interference intensifies"

2. **Bertrand et al. (2015)** - Gender Identity and Relative Income
   - Relationship: SST's resource preferences → **economic behavior**
   - Integration: Women's long-term resource strategy → marriage market penalties for high earners

## 💡 KEY QUOTES FOR DISSERTATION

### **Most Important Quote (Use in Chapter 3 Opening):**
> "This article proposes a contextual-evolutionary theory of human mating strategies. Both men and women are hypothesized to have evolved distinct psychological mechanisms that underlie short-term and long-term strategies... Different adaptive problems must be solved when pursuing a short-term as opposed to a long-term sexual strategy."

### **For Parental Investment Discussion (Chapter 2):**
> "Because men and women differ in minimum obligatory parental investment, men devote a larger proportion of their total mating effort than do women to short-term mating... An ancestral woman having sex with 100 men in 1 year could still not produce more than a single child. Thus, men are predicted to devote a larger share of their mating effort, compared with women, to sexual access to a variety of partners."

### **For Sexual Conflict Discussion (Chapter 8):**
> "The pursuit of a desire by one sex that interferes with the desires of the opposite sex is the major source of conflict between the sexes."

### **For Desire-Centered Framework (Chapter 3):**
> "Desire lies at the foundation of sexuality and human mating. The desires of one sex determine which tactics of attraction are effective when used by the opposite sex."

### **For Contextual Shifts (Chapter 7):**
> "In the long-term mating context men place a premium on sexual fidelity and abhor promiscuity in a woman... the reverse is true in a short-term mating context."
