### Annotation (if using Annotated Bibliography)

Thompson's qualitative study of OkCupid provides critical analysis of how digital dating platform design shapes relationship normativity. Through interviews with 57 consensually non-monogamous users and analysis of 45 profiles, Thompson demonstrates that while platforms increasingly include non-traditional relationship options, interface features (e.g., limiting profile linking to one partner, defaulting to singular "partner" language) still reinforce mononormative assumptions. This work is essential for understanding the technological dimension of the contemporary mating crisis and reveals how design choices can simultaneously empower and marginalize users. Thompson's findings suggest that addressing mating market dysfunction requires not just better technology, but fundamental reconsideration of assumptions embedded in digital architecture.
