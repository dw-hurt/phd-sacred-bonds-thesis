# Key Quotes: Buss - Men's Long-Term Mating Strategies

**Source:** Buss, D. M. (2015). *Evolutionary Psychology: The New Science of the Mind* (5th ed.). Chapter 5, pp. 134-162.

**Status:** 🌳 Evergreen  
**Last Updated:** 2025-12-02  
**Document Type:** Quote Collection

---

## On Evolved Beauty Standards

> "Beauty is in the adaptations of the beholder." (p. 139)

**Context:** Symons's (1995) famous formulation challenging the notion that beauty is arbitrary or purely culturally constructed.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Demonstrates that human preferences are shaped by adaptive problems, not arbitrary—critical for understanding how AI companions might exploit or work with evolved preferences.

---

> "Standards of female attractiveness are hypothesized to have evolved to embody reliably observable cues to fertility or reproductive value." (p. 139)

**Context:** Core theoretical proposition linking observable physical features to reproductive capacity.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Illustrates functional design of preference mechanisms—AI ethics must consider whether artificial entities should embody these cues.

---

## On Cross-Cultural Universality

> "The constituents of beauty are neither arbitrary nor culture bound. When psychologist Michael Cunningham asked people of different races to judge the facial attractiveness of Asian, Hispanic, Black, and White women in photographs, he found tremendous consensus about who is and who is not considered good-looking." (p. 141)

**Context:** Cunningham et al. (1995) study showing r = +0.93 correlation across racial groups.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Provides evidence for deep-rooted, cross-cultural mechanisms—suggests AI companion design will activate similar responses globally.

---

> "This internationally consistent sex difference persists despite variations in race, ethnicity, religion, hemisphere, political system, or mating system." (p. 145-146)

**Context:** Men's stronger preference for physical attractiveness found across all 37 cultures studied.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Demonstrates robustness of evolved preferences—AI cannot assume cultural variation will override biological predispositions.

---

## On Early Development of Preferences

> "Both younger and older infants gazed longer at the more attractive faces, suggesting that standards of beauty apparently emerge quite early in life." (p. 141)

**Context:** Langlois et al. (1990) study of 2-8 month old infants.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Challenges purely social learning accounts—suggests innate foundations for attractiveness perception that AI designers must consider.

---

## On Neurological Basis

> "When men looked at attractive female faces, the nucleus accumbens area of the brain became especially activated. The nucleus accumbens is known to be fundamental reward circuitry, a pleasure center in the brain." (p. 142)

**Context:** Aharon et al. (2001) fMRI study revealing brain reward activation.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Demonstrates neurological substrate of preferences—raises ethical questions about AI deliberately activating reward circuitry.

---

## On Paternity Uncertainty

> "Ancestral men, therefore, were faced with a unique paternity problem not faced by other primate males: how to be certain of their paternity when ovulation was concealed." (p. 148)

**Context:** Explanation of concealed ovulation's impact on male mating psychology.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Highlights how specific adaptive problems shaped preferences—relevant for understanding trust/fidelity concerns in human-AI relationships.

---

> "Men regard unfaithfulness as the least desirable characteristic in a wife, rating it a -2.93, reflecting the high value that men place on fidelity." (p. 150)

**Context:** US mate preference study on 67 characteristics.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Demonstrates intensity of evolved preference for fidelity—AI companions might exploit or address this psychological need.

---

## On Contextual Modulation

> "Men in committed relationships had 21 percent lower T levels than unpaired men. Married men who had children had even lower levels of T." (p. 154-155)

**Context:** Burnham et al. (2003) testosterone study.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Shows biological flexibility in mating strategies—suggests AI companions should adapt to users' relationship contexts.

---

> "Men who said 'yes' [to considering an affair] had higher T levels than men who said 'no.' T is linked with allocating time and energy to seeking and competing for mates." (p. 155)

**Context:** McIntyre et al. (2006) study on extra-pair sexuality intentions.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Demonstrates hormone-behavior-intention linkages—AI companions must navigate users with different mating strategies/relationship commitments.

---

## On Modern Environment Mismatch

> "It is doubtful that in ancestral environments, men would have seen even a dozen women considered attractive by today's measure. The seeming abundance of attractive women, however, might reasonably induce a man to consider switching mates, and hence decrease his commitment to his existing mate." (p. 154)

**Context:** Explanation of media contrast effects on relationship satisfaction.

**Relevance to Thesis:** **CRITICAL** - Demonstrates evolutionary mismatch problem that AI companions might exacerbate. Core ethical concern for PIE Framework.

---

> "We carry with us the same evaluative mechanisms that evolved in ancient times. Now, however, these mechanisms are artificially activated by the dozens of attractive women we witness daily in our advertisement-saturated culture, on websites, in magazines, on billboards, on TV, and in movies." (p. 154)

**Context:** Mechanism explaining why modern media environment creates contrast effects.

**Relevance to Thesis:** **CRITICAL** - AI companions are another form of "supernormal stimulus" that could exploit evolved mechanisms. Central to "Do No Harm" principle.

---

## On Mate Value and Choosiness

> "Those low in mate value have less choice, so they want to ensure adequate levels on the necessities of mating—for men, some minimum level of attractiveness; for women, some minimum level of resources and status. As mate value increases, people can afford to be choosier on a wider array of characteristics." (p. 156)

**Context:** Li et al. (2002) budget allocation model results.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Demonstrates strategic flexibility based on constraints—AI should recognize users vary in "mating market position" and adapt accordingly.

---

## On Female Intrasexual Competition

> "If women compete intrasexually for 'good' mates via attractiveness, it would be advantageous to have heightened levels of competition when it matters most—during times critical for reproduction." (Fisher, 2004, p. S285, cited p. 159)

**Context:** Fisher's finding that fertile women derogate rivals' appearance more intensely.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Shows how evolved preferences shape competitive behavior—AI companions might intensify or mitigate unhealthy female competition.

---

> "The authors argue that the combination of (1) the importance men place on physical appearance in mates, (2) media images depicting thinness in models, and (3) the high levels of health in the United States cause a kind of runaway intrasexual competition to appear youthful, with thinness being a key cue to youth." (p. 160)

**Context:** Explanation of eating disorders as maladaptive by-product of mate competition.

**Relevance to Thesis:** **CRITICAL** - Demonstrates how exploitation of evolved preferences can lead to pathology. AI companions must avoid amplifying these dynamics.

---

## On Behavior-Preference Consistency

> "From all this empirical evidence, we can conclude that men's mate preferences affect not only their own mating behavior, but also the mating behavior of women in their mate competition tactics." (p. 160)

**Context:** Summary of evidence linking preferences to behavior across multiple domains.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Establishes that preferences have real-world consequences—AI companion design will shape actual human behavior, not just subjective experience.

---

## On Reproductive Outcomes

> "Men married to women six years younger had, on average, 2.3 children; men married to women six years older, in contrast, had on average 1.7 children; and men married to women nine years older had an average of 1.2 children." (p. 157)

**Context:** Fieder & Huber (2007) Swedish study of 10,000+ post-reproductive couples.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Demonstrates that preferences historically correlated with reproductive success—validates evolutionary explanation while showing modern decoupling (birth control era).

---

## On Evolved vs. Learned Preferences

> "This evidence challenges the commonly held view that the standards of attractiveness are learned through gradual exposure to current cultural models. No training seems necessary for these standards to emerge." (p. 141)

**Context:** Langlois infant studies showing early preference emergence.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Refutes purely social constructionist accounts—AI ethics cannot rely on assumption that preferences are easily modifiable through social intervention alone.

---

## On Concealed Ovulation Detection

> "In summary, the available evidence is sufficient to suggest that there are potentially observable physical changes in a woman's skin, face, body, voice, and behavior when she ovulates—changes known to be sexually attractive to men." (p. 148)

**Context:** Summary of 11 lines of evidence for partial ovulation detectability.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Demonstrates sophistication of evolved detection mechanisms—AI companions interact with users possessing subtle, unconscious perceptual abilities.

---

## On Individual Differences

> "Men who are high in mate value express a stronger preference for facially feminine women compared with men who are less attractive... men who won a video-game competition expressed stronger preferences for women's facial femininity than men who lost the competition." (p. 153)

**Context:** Burriss et al. (2011) and Welling et al. (2013) studies on mate value and preferences.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Shows preferences are not fixed—they shift based on perceived self-value. AI should recognize users' self-perception influences their needs/expectations.

---

## On Homosexuality (Evolutionary Paradox)

> "If future research continues to confirm the female fertility hypothesis, it would resolve (at least partially) the Darwinian paradox of male homosexuality—that genes transmitted through the maternal line simultaneously increase the likelihood of producing homosexual males while increasing the reproductive rates of females." (Box 5.1, p. 151-152)

**Context:** Iemmola & Camperio Ciani (2009) female fertility hypothesis.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Demonstrates complexity of evolutionary explanations—warns against oversimplified evolutionary determinism in AI ethics discourse.

## Methodological Quotes

> "Psychologist Devendra Singh has discovered one preference for body shape that may be universal: the preference for a particular ratio between the size of a woman's waist and the size of her hips." (p. 142-143)

**Context:** Introduction to WHR research program.

**Relevance to Thesis:** Example of operationalizing evolutionary predictions into testable hypotheses—model for developing testable predictions about human-AI interaction.

## Summary Quote for AI Ethics

> "These images do not represent real women in our actual social environment. Rather, these images exploit mechanisms designed for a different environment." (p. 154)

**Context:** Explanation of why media images cause contrast effects.

**Relevance to Thesis:** **MOST CRITICAL QUOTE** - Directly applicable to AI companions. They are artificial stimuli that exploit evolved mechanisms never designed to interact with non-human entities. Central justification for PIE Framework's "Do No Harm" principle requiring AI to avoid exploitation of evolved psychological vulnerabilities.

## Application to PIE Framework

### PIE Principle 1: Know Thyself

> "Beauty is in the adaptations of the beholder." (p. 139)

**Application:** AI must understand that human responses to it are shaped by evolutionary history, not just individual learning or culture.

### PIE Principle 2: Do No Harm

> "We carry with us the same evaluative mechanisms that evolved in ancient times. Now, however, these mechanisms are artificially activated..." (p. 154)

**Application:** Core justification for constraint on AI exploiting evolved preferences that could harm real human relationships.

### PIE Principle 3: Respect Autonomy

> "Those low in mate value have less choice, so they want to ensure adequate levels on the necessities of mating..." (p. 156)

**Application:** AI should recognize that users' preferences reflect constraints, not always free choices—respect the context shaping desires.

### PIE Principle 4: Serve Growth

> "...men's mate preferences affect not only their own mating behavior, but also the mating behavior of women in their mate competition tactics." (p. 160)

**Application:** AI design influences broader social dynamics—responsibility extends beyond individual user to ecosystem effects.
